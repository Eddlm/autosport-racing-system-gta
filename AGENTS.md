4. `ApplySteerLimits` (`Racer.cs:961`) — the two side limits closed by a single `Clamp`, and it runs **last among the writers of the steering request**. 5. `ConvertSpeedToPedals` (`Racer.cs:1072`) — one `[-1,1]` combined input, split, then the **reason-cap compose** (also pre-slew) and the slew. **GridWait lives in three places** (`ComputeTargetSpeed`, `ProcessAI` handbrake pulses, `ComputeSteering` hold-straight) — keep them in sync.

## The steering authority chain (`ComputeSteering`)
**The aim point is the whole geometry**: the lane steer is pure pursuit — the bearing from the car's travel direction to the aim point, its curvature `2 sin(bearing)/range` times the wheelbase (`PursuitSteerFromBearing` `Racer.cs`), with pure pursuit's factor of two carried in `PursuitGain`. The **course error is deleted**: referenced to the road's direction at the lookahead node it over-commanded by roughly `lookahead/radius`, which *was* the corner cutting, and the bearing already carries that information. **Off-track recovery is deleted**: off-track it and the lane call were the same law at the same point at gain 1 each, so it doubled the command rather than recovering anything distinct, and the lane pursuit alone recovers the car — driver-verified, and it can oscillate because the car keeps enough speed to overshoot. Then rival repulsion **added** to the lane steer and never overwriting it, the side-by-side heading match (**one-way** — only while the overlapping rival moves toward this car's side, `Racer.cs:564`), and the **yaw damper referenced to the aim point's required yaw** — the loop's only rate feedback, so **it is load-bearing: with it off the cars limit-cycle around centre**, and against *zero* it taxed every steady corner into a standing lateral offset.

**No term is gated** — the old `|targetLane|` guard is gone, so the pursuit runs whenever `TryGetSteerContext` passes; `ApplySteerLimits` and the slew rate only cap the output. Full analysis, and why the aim-error PID was tried, driven and **abandoned** in favour of this chain: `AGENTS-STEERING.md`.

## Lane systems — where on the track the car aims
Resolved in `ComputeSteering`, later overriding earlier: **high-speed line** (`Racer.cs` `ComputeHighSpeedLane`, runs every tick and takes the inside edge only where the node a lookahead ahead carries a radius under the gate), **corner outside approach** (`Racer.cs` `ComputeCornerTargetLane`, a temporary outside hold), **avoidance** (`Racer.cs` `ComputeAvoidAheadLane`), then **`ApplyRivalWalls`** (`Racer.cs`) clamps the result between track bounds and rival walls.

**Lane systems only *set* the target lane; how hard it is pursued is the authority chain's job.** Every lane target insets by half the car's width — the inside line and the avoidance bound alike — except the outside hold, which asks for the raw half width and relies on the aim clamp to pull it in; `OutOfTrackDistance` (`Racer.cs:2003`) is centre-based on the raw half width, which is why a car pushed off is judged by its centre.

- **Two steering lookaheads, only one of them named — do not conflate them**: `ComputeHighSpeedLane`'s chord is anonymous, while `LookAhead.SteerRef` — which feeds every lane bound, the aim point and the debug line — is a **preview time** (`SteerPreviewSeconds` `Racer.cs`), floored and capped in metres, rather than a fixed distance, because a fixed distance raises the loop's natural frequency with speed while the actuator lag stays put. `SteerLookaheadMinMeters` is its floor and is **live** again.
- **The outside hold is the car's own per-corner decision**: `UpdateCornerRequirements` (`Racer.cs:2713`) flags each corner for braking and/or an outside position from its predicted arrival against its own apex speed, and the hold engages only with the positioning flag set while arriving near that apex speed (`Racer.cs:710`). **The hold decision can flip to "hold" once per approach but never back** (`Racer.cs:714`).
- **A sweeping corner suppresses its own outside hold through the positioning decision**: the flag requires a tight corner (`Racer.cs:2734`) and route speed governs a sweeper's speed either way. That regime is no longer switchable — the Route Speed Limit toggle went in `70e177c` — so the lever that shifts it is **Route Offset (mph)**.
- **Corner-commit maneuvers** replace the outside hold with a lane beside the target rival on the corner's inside, and clean up when their apex is passed. The corner line phases are a **hard switch**, no lerp.
- **The lane pursuit was gated on the target's offset, not the car's error, and the gate is now REMOVED** — the pursuit runs whenever `TryGetSteerContext` passes, so a straight or gentle sweep (where `ComputeHighSpeedLane` returns zero) now drives the same pursuit on the car's own live offset instead of getting no lane steer at all. The guard was a leftover of the error-based lane-bias era, when "guidance" meant a lane request or a rival wall (`dcef913`); the gate removal has **not** had its own drive.
- **A lane becomes a steer by pure pursuit off its aim point** — the bearing from the car's travel direction to `steerRefPoint.Position + steerRight * targetLane`, turned into an angle by the wheelbase (`PursuitSteerDegrees` `Racer.cs`); **no radius produces a steer**, and every lane source feeds it. The inside line aims at the **inside edge**, and the steer carries pure pursuit's factor of two (`PursuitGain`, `Racer.cs`); a target that steps from wherever the car is instead holds a constant error and never converges. Outward moves scale off a full inside hug — the hold at three of them, since its error is the whole half-width and equal strength still loses to the corner's own course error. The ordering is load-bearing — car's-heading-to-the-aim is the steering command's own convention, and a minus inverts the lane. **Do not "fix" an aggressive lane move with a ceiling**: a ceiling flattens any lane law into a constant-command phase that builds lateral velocity the short tail cannot shed, and that *is* the overshoot — the lookahead's length and the step into the line are the levers (`AGENTS-TECHNOTES.md`). **The aim offset carries a Gs-aware preview**: its lateral coordinate shifts by half the car's projected lateral motion (`ProjectAhead` `Racer.cs`), so the command leads the drift instead of reacting to it. It is zero at steady state, so it moves the lane-change transient and never the steady gain — and it therefore steers *earlier still* when the car is being pushed away from its target, which is the case that falsifies "it reels back aggression" as a general claim. **The aim offset is clamped to the drivable edge** (raw half-width less half the car, not the raw edge the rival walls bound to), because the preview shift is applied after the wall clamp and could otherwise aim the car off the track. **With no lane demand the aim is the car's own live offset**, so a straight holds its line rather than chasing the road centre; the flip back to the centre once off the surface is now the only thing that turns an off-track car around, and the preview only damps the drift the hold gives up rather than bounding it.

## Speed pipeline — how fast the car goes
`Intention.Speed` is `min(cornerSpd + Corner Offset, followTrackSpd + Route Offset)`, then clamped by `MaxSpeed` and the off-track projection cap. **The offsets are split, one per authority, and each is applied inside the `min`** — so Corner Offset also lifts apex-limited speed (and `CorneringSpeedLimit`, `Racer.cs:1438`) while apex speed itself stays pure physics (`AutosportRacingSystem.cs:2417`).

- **`cornerSpd`** — the kinematic braking plan toward the apex speed over the distance to the apex node (`Racer.cs:2433`). Its decel (`Racer.cs:2469`) is a grip-limited base **plus the gravity component of the mean grade over the braking span**, sampled along the same trimmed span the solve integrates. **A crest before the corner is in that decel too**: `CornerPoint` carries a crest node, its curvature at a fixed probe and the extent its unload ramps over (`AssignCrestNodes` `TrackLoader.cs`), the solve scales **only the grip-limited base** by the clipped-triangle unload (`CrestDecelFactor` `Racer.cs`) because vertical curvature is a normal-load effect while grade is along-slope gravity, and the entrance walks back to the crest top so the plan starts while the car is loaded.
- **Gs and degrees are not interchangeable, and a threshold censors its own evidence.** `HillGripDeltaGs` returns speed-scaled Gs (`v²κ/g`) while a pitch break is pure geometry, so one feature reads a couple of degrees and a couple of G depending on unit and window — a launch test must name the speed it assumes. A scan that stops at its threshold can never log what sits below it, so "the distribution shows a clean gap" is not evidence the threshold is safe; log the unthresholded value first.
- **`followTrackSpd`** — route curvature from a time-based lookahead window (`Racer.cs:2394` sampled, `Racer.cs:2772` the law), the authority for sweeping corners, and **always in the `min`**: the Route Speed Limit toggle that could drop it to 999 was removed in `70e177c`, replaced by the additive Route Offset. Its one live modifier is the **`SteerLimitedSpeed` blend** (`Racer.cs:1420`, 70% steer-implied speed to 30% route speed) and it is fed the *clamped* steer of the previous pass, so a tighter steer ceiling **raises** route speed.
- **Slope grip loss is LIVE** (exponential pitch model on corner + route speed) — ignore any older note claiming otherwise. The hill/crest grip floors are tuned as one knob; read the code for current values, and note the known issue that the model reads an upcoming hill and brakes before the car reaches it.
- **Pedal gain is one linear map, no feedforward** (`Racer.cs:1119`), with the brake side divided by `BrakeErrorMultiplier` on top because braking is deliberately the softer side. Both pedals then ramp to that target at a fixed slew rate.
- **Throttle limits are per-reason (`1ebbd2c`)**: every limiter owns a `MaxThrottleFrom<X>` level field in `UpdateThrottleReasonCaps` (`Racer.cs:1530`), and `ComposeThrottleCap` (`Racer.cs:1618`) takes `min(base, reasons)` **pre-slew**, naming the winner into `ThrottleReason` — the standing answer to "why isn't the pedal full", read off the bar sphere wearing the winner's colour. The fields cut at the shared rate and recover at **half** it (`GlideCap` `Racer.cs:1523`), the brake side mirrors them (`UpdateBrakeReasonCaps` `Racer.cs:1598`, `ComposeBrakeCap` `Racer.cs:1646`), and the composed `MaxThrottle`/`MaxBrake` are no longer drawn. Overrides (grid wait, countersteer, stuck recovery, offtrack) re-tag after.
- **The overspeed reason is the incline-boost anti-cheat**: it compares measured forward Gs against the **total** wheel-pushed Gs with a deadband (`OverspeedExcessGs` `Racer.cs:3174`), so it fires whenever the car accelerates harder than its wheels are asking. It arms only on a straight line above its speed gate (`Racer.cs:1540`), which deliberately leaves the launch alone — the engine's assist there is exactly the excess it would otherwise read.
- **Off-track projection cap (`Racer.cs:1148`)** caps the combined input by how far off-centre the projection lands, ramping across the whole half-width to a shallow floor. It is applied as a `Min`, so it never cuts braking — but a negative cap **is** a brake command over the plan's own throttle.
- **The throttle/brake avoidance override was REMOVED.** **Asymmetry is by design** — corner is the loose target, route the tight one, so don't "balance" them with flat offsets; on high-grip cars the route term wins.

## Corner lifecycle (apex table + three held targets)
1. **Apex table at generation** — `BuildApexTable` (`TrackLoader.cs:228`) detects corner regions on the smoothed radius and `AddCornerRegion` takes each region's **smoothed minimum** as its apex, keeping the region's smallest precise radius alongside it as the 1g speed's radius; two apexes the car reaches close in time are one complex, and so is a pair whose exit and entrance aim the same way inside a short window — that branch catches a fast kink the car clears before it can react to the slow corner behind it, so the window must stay under the time a U-turn's two apexes take to be planned separately, or the second absorbs the first and the merged span never covers the lead-in. It keeps the **slower by smoothed radius** and re-walks the survivor's span. **Both span ends derive from the apex**, walked outward to where the smoothed radius opens past a multiple of the apex's own — entrance tighter than exit so the two ends cannot chatter — bounded by the region the detector already found and by a node cap that stays a node count, because a radius multiple there gave a sweeper twice its own radius. **The apex is the smoothed minimum, not the region's tightest node** — a ±4-node chord measures a wide corner below the file's own decimal quantum. A region must also run a minimum length and carry a tight enough apex to register at all, which is what drops the blips and shallow bends; no radius flag survives, and the racer decides per corner (`UpdateCornerRequirements` `Racer.cs:2713`).
2. **Held apex queue per racer** — `UpdateApexLeapfrog` (`Racer.cs:2670`) prunes stale entries and leapfrogs passed ones each tick, while `RefillApexQueue` (`Racer.cs:2756`) **derives the queue fresh on every refill**: slot 1 is always the nearest corner ahead, the rest the most restrictive corners this car wants a braking plan for; the queue invalidates on a route-data change or a low-speed incident. Per-car apex speed is physics, `√(grip · gravity · radius)`.
3. **Braking map** — every held apex the car has flagged for braking feeds a kinematic solve over the usable distance and `cornerSpd` takes the lowest demand (`Racer.cs:1381`); its decel is grip-limited times a **per-apex learned factor**, seeded per racer per corner and corrected from the share of the braking phase spent at full pedal, with a flat cut for a corner taken with a real slide (`CommitBrakeLearning` `Racer.cs:1279`). The buffer is a flat time × speed, pressure does **not** scale it, and **a car whose ABS holds the pedal down reads as under-braking and drifts its factor up** — the learner watches pedal share, not achieved decel.
4. **Route-speed split** — route speed governs sweeping corners while the three-apex map handles tight ones from far away, and **min governs** (`Racer.cs:1448`). **Braking anchors on the corner entrance less a flat lead** (`BrakeTargetBaseNode`), which retires the turn-in lead that scaled with speed and radius — the same node the release and the learned factor's sampler use, so plan, release and learning all name one node — and the apex plan is released only once braking is genuinely done, down at apex speed (`Racer.cs:1398`).
5. The old live rolling and far-killer scans are **superseded**, but `Brain.Corner` is **live again**: `CommitApexQueue` (`Racer.cs:2848`) re-instances it each tick from the nearest held apex, so the outside hold, the commit lane and the maneuver gates read live state.

Track facts: node counts are treated as metres; measured spacing on `Airport Quick 1` averaged 1.034 m/node over 1491 gaps (0.877–2.394 m), so the convention is count-as-metres, not exact spacing. Circuit lookaheads use modulo and point-to-point clamps.

## Per-racer state
- **Aggression** (grid-assigned by position, first lowest to last highest; the player stays mid) scales only the avoidance buffer — the old note that it scaled allowed TCS wheelspin is retired: `TcsCapLevel` reads no aggression. **Pressure** is proximity × aggression, rising slowly and falling quickly, and it drives divebomb/defend/arming.
- **Instability owns the throttle cut now, and it is a first cut (`Racer.cs:1556`)**: the 3 Hz `WheelsOnGround` latch, `AirborneThrottleLevel` and `MaxThrottleFromStability` are **removed**, and `IsUnstable` instead reads every tick, firing when the chassis rides above the at-rest height captured in `Launch` or when its yaw rate demands more lateral acceleration than the known grip allows. `CurrentMechanicalGrip` still carries no stability factor, and nothing models roll, pitch, load or suspension.
- **Maneuvers are cards** (`Maneuver` on the racer, no legacy arm blocks): a card **plays** into the slot and **folds** on its own condition, priority **ChillOut → DefendLane → DiveBomb → Yield**, with **Nitro slotless**. A played card is **never reconsidered mid-play** — unplaying causes thrash.
- **ChillOut** halves throttle and holds station behind the closest rival ahead until the field thins, arming only above a minimum speed so a slow car cannot become a roadblock. **DiveBomb** targets the closest reachable rival with a deeper braking target while its card lives.
- **DefendLane** covers the inside against a faster chaser, and **Yield** lets a faster overlapping rival by near the entrance. The corner-commit lane (`Racer.cs:727`) engages inside the outside-approach window and ignores the one-way hold latch and the per-corner positioning decision while the card lives.
- **Nitro** has four situations (contested / defended / lonely / finish-spender) behind vetoes on braking, off-track, big steer angle and low gear on high-geared RWD cars. **AWD is exempt** via the handling struct's drive bias; full rationale in `AGENTS-TECHNOTES.md`. The player fires via key press (`TryFireNitrous` `Racer.cs`), the AI via `TryPlayNitrousCard`; both share one shot per lap, the same charge cycle, and the same `SetOverrideNitrousLevel` native path.
- **Rule of thumb: the player and the AI follow the same rules.** When adding a mechanic, default to one code path for both; diverge only when the game engine forces it (e.g. the player's special ability, the AI's free ABS).
- **Passengerize** shifts AI drivers to the passenger seat while a rival overlaps, so they cannot swerve into contact, and is never applied to the player. **Ghosting was removed** in the avoidance cleanup and must be rebuilt later.
- **The player's car and the AI's get different engine systems** — the special ability writes a real per-wheel grip multiplier and a faster steering ramp while it slows the world clock, `Automobile.cpp`'s player-only block adds a sideslip auto-centre, and the AI side gets free ABS and time-sliced wheel collisions; ARS neither detects nor blocks any of it (`AGENTS-VANILLA-STEERING.md`).

**Duel model (designed via Council, not implemented) — full design in `AGENTS-DUEL.md`.** Physics-aware card play on one shared time-to-apex primitive taken from each car's own live plan; **anything about rivals, overtaking or card play starts in `AGENTS-DUEL.md`.**

## Durable gotchas — do not "fix" these
- **`if (1 == 2) return;` is an INVERTED gate — it disables nothing**, because the return only fires when the condition is true and it never is. Correct idioms are a bare `return;` or the block form; this bit the start-line flare disable (`bdd1b29`) for weeks.
- **Remap with a descending output range + `clamp=true` is inverted** by `Clamp` when `min > max`, and NaN compares less-than-anything. Keep output clamps ascending and use a descending *input* range for a reversed map.
- **NaN discipline**: `Clamp(NaN, -limit, +limit)` returns the min bound, i.e. instant full-lock — guard steering outputs and any clamp input that can be non-finite. Never hand a non-value sentinel to a *seeding* getter either, or it writes the sentinel into the ini.
- **A null-check on a freshly `new`-ed object is dead code.** Two "cannot find file" popups lived behind `if (new XmlDocument() == null)` while the real failures *throw*, so those paths have **no** error handling.
- Synchronous setup work can pause `OnTick` and temporarily suppress per-frame debug visuals; make it incremental if that matters.
- **`Handling.Downforce` reads `0x0014` in `VehicleMemory.GetDownforce` (`VehicleMemory.cs:34`) and the code is right** — an earlier note claiming `0x0010` was a transcription slip off an off-by-one source line number. With the right field the principled grip divider matches the engine's own formula, so don't bring back the old curve-fit hack.
- **Oversteer and sliding are different quantities, and what a steering law can see depends entirely on its reference vector** — never state a reference's consequences as general properties. The live pursuit bearing is velocity-referenced, which is the *blind* one: for a pure body rotation it reads as perfectly tracked **while the car is sideways**, and the slide authority is the blend instead.
- **The aim-error PID / measured-yaw-rate steering law was tried, driven and ABANDONED — the pre-PID chain was restored in preference to it.** Do not resurrect it blind; the durable lesson is that **it had no cross-track term at all**, so its standing line error could only be fought with P. Full record in `AGENTS-STEERING.md`.
- **Offroad gravity needs a baseline reset before the multiplier** — `Initialize` (`Racer.cs:323`) runs every race and on respawn, so setting gravity immediately before the offroad multiply is load-bearing or it stacks across restarts.
- **Speed asymmetry is intentional** (see the Speed pipeline) — don't "fix" it. **`Intention.SteerLimitedSpeed` is live** (`Racer.cs:1420`, blended 70/30 into `followTrackSpd`), and it is fed the *clamped* steer of the previous pass — the older note here claiming it was write-only was wrong.
- **`Options` enum values are positional**, so retiring a member renumbers the rest and one must never be persisted or exchanged as an int. Nothing does today, but re-check before any numeric consumer appears.
- **`World.DrawMarker` has a per-frame render budget**: markers past it are **silently not drawn** and draw order decides who loses, so long debug geometry belongs in `DRAW_LINE` instead. The budget is shared with every other marker the script draws that frame, so a visual can break because something *else* got greedy.
- **Two traps the flare rebuild exposed, both reusable**: a prop's own `RightVector` is perpendicular to *that prop's* forward, so an offset applied after its heading is turned 90° comes out parallel to the track — offset from the source direction instead; and a **named ptfx asset streams between frames**, so requesting it in a same-frame loop can never succeed — queue the entity and attach it from a tick (`TrackLoader.TickQueuedFlares`), logging a failed start rather than swallowing it.
- **Do not go hunting for an old limiter that used the static TRlat — the ceiling never reads it.** `Handling.LateralTractionCurve` is only the *input* to the speed-scaled peak (`LateralPeakAtSpeed`, `Racer.cs:900`) plus the countersteer gate thresholds, the slide blend and the TCS spin map, and every limiter function (`AckermannCeilingDegrees`, `GeometrySteerCeiling`, `PeakSlipCeilingAt`, `ResolveSteerCeiling`, `ApplySteerLimits`) has a caller.
- **A POSITIVE steer command steers LEFT — do not re-derive this.** `Racer.cs`'s own lane law gives it away (`laneSteerDeg = -laneErrorMeters * laneGain` against the `+ = right` **lane** convention, which is a different convention), the rival repulsion and corner-commit lane agree, and `TrackPoint.Angle` is already in the steer's convention (a left-hand corner is positive), so a reference built from it must be used as-is. `SteerLimitLeft` bounds positive commands and `SteerLimitRight` negative ones, which is what `Racer.cs`'s clamp now reads (`92d5867`), while the Show Inputs fan draws each limit on its true side — orange left, red right. A rename here is a *side* swap, not a symbol swap, so check every use by side rather than by name.
