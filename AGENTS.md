## The per-frame pipeline (Racer, timed core)
Rough order, each step consuming the last:
1. `UpdateTrackPosition` — local ±6-node search (global rescan past ~25 m), builds time-based lookaheads (0.25s→2s), computes the route radii, ticks laps.
2. `ComputeTargetSpeed` — builds the **intended speed** (see Speed pipeline) and sets `_cornerSpd` = current corner's apex speed.
3. `ComputeSteering` — resolves the lane (see Lane systems), derives heading/lane/recovery angles, runs the PD to `Control.SteerDegrees`. **The heading-error term is load-bearing — do not remove or merge it into the lane term.** It anchors the car to face the track direction, which is what catches lateral-drift overshoot at the lane center. Merging heading + lane into a single "aim at the point" angle was tried and reverted: without a separate heading term the car sails past the lane and oscillates.
4. `ApplySteerLimits` — speed-based clamp on steer (only when steer and yaw share sign).
5. `ConvertSpeedToPedals` — the speed loop: intended speed → a single `[-1,1]` combined input, then split to throttle/brake and capped/slewed. No reverse routing — the pedal pipeline is forward/brake only; reverse exists solely in the stuck-recovery override. The old throttle/brake avoidance override (`_accelerationCap`) was removed. **GridWait no longer lives here** — it is handled by `ComputeTargetSpeed` (pins `Intention.Speed`), `ProcessAI` (refreshes handbrake), and `TranslateSteerToInput`/`TryGetSteerContext` (skips steering). Keep these three places in sync if GridWait behavior changes.
6. `TranslateSteerToInput` — smooths steer toward the target and remaps degrees → -1..1 input.
7. Traction control, maneuvers, stuck recovery run around these.

## Lane systems — where on the track the car aims
Resolved in `ComputeSteering` in this override order (later overrides earlier):
1. **System 2 — high-speed line (`ComputeHighSpeedLane`)**: always runs. Positions the car on the **inside edge of the track's own curvature**, derived purely from track geometry (signed angle between the current track direction and a speed-scaled lookahead node ahead) — fully independent of the corner system. Disabled past a radius ceiling (wider = effectively straight).
2. **System 1 — corner outside approach (`ComputeCornerTargetLane`)**: temporary override. Holds the **outside line** only during the approach window before a key corner's apex, then lets go (`0f`) so System 2's inside takes over naturally.
3. **Avoidance (`ComputeAvoidAheadLane`)**: overrides both when a rival ahead needs passing.
4. **`ApplyRivalWalls`**: clamps the final lane between track bounds and rival walls. **Invariants: the car's full width always stays on track; the buffer always includes the average of both cars' bounding boxes (no physical contact possible) + an aggression extra.** Overlap classification uses an asymmetric longitudinal window by design: more margin from a rival ahead (the car moves off them earlier), nearly none from one just passed (it crosses back over to the racing line as soon as it's clear).

Design rules:
- **Gain discipline**: each system has a pursuit gain (avoidance fixed; corner systems radius-scaled). The gain scales how *hard* the target is pursued — the *target itself* is always a track edge. Aggressive steer-**out** is dangerous, so the outside approach is deliberately gentler than the inside commit.
- **Corner approach**: only engages if the car entered the window needing to brake (entry latch); let-go time scales with track width (wider track → release earlier, more time to cross to the inside). The outside-hold decision is made once per approach, keyed to the apex node, and resets after the apex is passed so each lap re-decides. It can flip from "no hold" to "hold" if the car accelerates into the braking window after a slow entry (e.g., standing-start first corner).
- **Corner-commit maneuvers** (Divebomb / DefendLane): inside the outside-approach window they replace the outside hold with a lane right beside the target rival on the corner's inside — the divebomber to out-brake an ahead rival, the defender to block a behind rival from diving. Same lane formula, opposite target frame; both clean up when their armed apex is passed (defend also drops on overtake).
- The corner line phases are a **hard switch** (outside → inside), no lerp.

## Gs-aware preview steering (lane error blend)
- **The concept**: pure-pursuit lane steering measures the lane error at the *car's current position*, implicitly assuming the path follows the nose. At speed, centrifugal force (already present in `ProjectAhead`, whose acceleration term is measured, not commanded) curves the real path, so nose-aimed steering overshoots. The preview fix: measure the lane error at the **1s Gs-aware projection** instead — steering then anticipates centrifugal drift rather than reacting to it.
- **Implementation** (in `ComputeSteering`'s lane-bias block): the lane error is a blend of the legacy error (measured at the car) and the preview error (measured at `ProjectAhead(1f)` against the `LookAhead.OneSec` node frame). Blend 0 = exact legacy behavior (the toggle is the A/B switch); blend 1 = full preview.
- **Lane-bias scale**: the raw `atan2(laneError, lookaheadDist)` pure-pursuit angle is divided by 2 before becoming the steer target. The divisor-100 exp-gain curve (`_laneGainDivisor`, `LaneGainExponent`) was **removed** — it crushed every realistic lane-target angle to a few tenths of a degree (the divisor's 100 was tuned for a 0-100° mental model that doesn't match the actual magnitudes). `/2` makes the steer target represent the lane move's *average* heading over the lookahead window, not the instantaneous angle. Per-car variation via `_laneGainDivisor` (90-110) was also lost — accept that all cars steer identically to lane targets for now; revisit if the field feels too uniform.
- **Menu**: Debug submenu — "Gs-Aware Preview" checkbox (`Options.GsAwarePreview` in `DebugToggles`) plus a "Preview Blend" list item (steps between 0.1 and 1.0) writing `ARS.GsAwarePreviewBlend`. Default off. (`LaneGainExponent` menu item is **dormant** — the curve it controlled no longer exists; kept for save compatibility, can be re-purposed if a new curve is added.)
- The projection read in steering is one frame stale (`RunTimedCore` runs before `ProcessTick` refreshes `AccelerationVector`) — same staleness the projection consumers live with; acceptable by design.

## Speed pipeline — how fast the car goes
- **`cornerSpd`** — the "ballpark": kinematic braking plan `√(v²+2·a·d)` targeting the **apex speed**, distance to the **apex node**, plus a flat +5 m/s offset.
- **`followTrackSpd`** — the "precise": route curvature `√(g·grip·radius)` from a time-based lookahead window (three circumradius sample points), plus a flat offset and a bidirectional pressure offset. The window is measured in seconds ahead, not scaled by grip, so it responds consistently at all speeds.
- **Slope grip loss is LIVE** (exponential pitch model, applied to both corner and route speed in `ComputeTargetSpeed`). It was commented out for tuning and later re-enabled — ignore any older note claiming otherwise.
- **Hill/crest grip floors**: `HillGripMin` (slope model, `AutosportRacingSystem.cs`) and the two crest/dip vertical grip factors (route + corner, `ComputeTargetSpeed`) are tuned together as one knob — floored at 0.8 for a long stretch, then lowered to 0.1. **Read the code for the current value; don't trust this file.** (With no/low floor, sharp crests could zero out corner speed.)
- **`Intention.Speed` = min(cornerSpd, followTrackSpd)**, then clamped by engine top speed, `MaxSpeed`, and the off-track projection cap (`ComputeOffshootSpeedCap`). Both `cornerSpd` and `followTrackSpd` receive a `+0 to +4 m/s` pressure speed bias (mapped from pressure 0–100) before the min is taken.
- **Pedal gain**: combined input = speed error (m/s) divided by a full-pedal error divisor (code: `FullPedalSpeedErrorMps`) — full pedal at that error, linear in between; the value lives in code. The old Gs-based gain (`* 1f` on Gs) caused under-braking through long braking zones.
- **Off-track speed cap**: `ComputeOffshootSpeedCap` — when the 1 s projection falls outside the safe bound on a corner's outside, intended speed is remapped between a grip-scaled floor and no-cap over the off-track distance. An instant clamp, not an incremental decay.
- **Throttle/brake avoidance override**: **REMOVED**. The old `_accelerationCap` field (±1 input-domain scalar) was scaffolding but no system ever pulled it down; it only created confusion. Avoidance/reaction to rivals and rear-end prevention will be rebuilt from scratch when needed.
- **Asymmetry is by design**: corner is the loose target, route is the tight one. Do not add flat offsets to `followTrackSpd` to "balance" them.
- **High-grip cars don't respond to braking-side tuning**: their braking is so strong that the braking plan rarely binds — route-curvature speed control wins. Adjustments that act on the braking plan barely register on them; tune the route-speed side instead.

## Corner lifecycle (precomputed apex table + four held targets)
1. **Apex table at generation time** (`BuildApexCorners`, end of `GenerateRouteInfo`): a second pass walks the track **sequentially in chunks**; in each chunk the node with the **smallest precise curve radius** is the chunk's apex, kept **only if its radius is under a corner limit**. Each `CornerPoint` in `ARS.Corners` carries only the node, that radius, and a tentative 1g corner speed (`√(g·r)`). Nothing else — no spans, angles, thresholds. (Earlier local-minimum criteria over ±1 then ±4 neighbors produced ~every node as a corner on noisy recorded tracks; the chunked pass fixed it.) **Close-corner rule**: a corner within 30 nodes of the previous one is dropped outright (the two apexes read as one); 30–100 nodes apart it is kept but marked `SuppressOutsideApproach` — racers never hold the outside line (nor corner-commit) into it, since the approach fights over the same braking window. **Gentle-bend rule**: corners with an apex radius over the gentle-bend ceiling are also kept but flagged (they were previously dropped outright) — the high-speed line governs them. `ComputeCornerTargetLane` matches the flag by apex node against `ARS.Corners` (both instancing paths build synthetic `CornerPoint`s, so the flag is looked up, not copied).
2. **Held apex queue per racer** (`UpdateNextApexes`): targets persist until passed, then leapfrog forward and refill by scanning after the last held apex. Redundant close apexes are skipped unless materially slower; the queue invalidates when route data changes or the car suffers a low-speed incident. Per-car apex speed = `√(grip · gravity · radius)`.
3. **Braking map (ApexBrakingSpeed)**: each held apex feeds the kinematic map `v = √(vApex² + 2·a·d)` over the usable distance (apex distance minus a buffer of `ApexBufferSeconds` × corner speed). `cornerSpd = min(map(apex1..4))` — whichever corner demands the lowest speed wins. The buffer is pressure-scaled (0 pressure = brake earlier, 100 = later) and divebomb shortens it further.
4. **Route-speed split**: the near window (three sample points over a fixed time-ahead span, circumradius through them) gauges `followTrackSpd` and is the authority for **sweeping corners**; the four-apex braking map handles tight ("killer") corners from far away. `Intention.Speed = min(cornerSpd, followTrackSpd)`. The route speed is **no longer gated off** near the apex — both speeds always run and the min governs (the old 2s gate caused unawareness in complex curves). **Corner-map handover is positional and unconditional**: the route speed takes over the corner the moment the braking target (entrance node) is passed — no speed condition of any kind. During the approach the apex braking map keeps full authority (an mph-delta handover let cars run a fixed margin over the plan and brake way too late at angled entrances; a later +Δ-margin condition let the corner map linger mid-corner and caused braking inside corners). The route speed governs inside the corner itself.
5. The old live per-racer rolling scan (`FindNextCorner`) and the far-killer 4s→8s window scan are **superseded** — no code instancing corners from live perception remains. However, `Brain.Corner` itself is **live again**: `CommitApexQueue` re-instances it every tick from the nearest held apex (`NextApexNode`), so the outside-approach lane, divebomb/defend, and chevron consumers read live corner state sourced from the apex queue. New consumers may read either; the queue carries more (all four held apexes, radii, per-car speeds).

Track facts: **1 node = 1 m**. Circuit lookaheads use modulo; point-to-point clamps.

## Grid car selection (pace-matched)
- **Vehicle stats can be read from the model without spawning**: model-level natives take an **int model hash**, not a spawned handle. Confirmed-working set: `GET_VEHICLE_MODEL_MAX_TRACTION` (`0x539DE94D44FDFD0D`, grip in G), `_GET_VEHICLE_MODEL_ESTIMATED_TOP_SPEED` (`0xF417C2502FFFED43`, m/s), `GET_VEHICLE_MODEL_ACCELERATION` (`0x8C044C5C84505B6A`), `GET_VEHICLE_MODEL_MAX_BRAKING` (`0xDC53FD41B4ED944C`). The spawned-handle versions are *different* natives (e.g. `GET_VEHICLE_ACCELERATION` / `0x53AF99BAA671CA47`, `GET_VEHICLE_MAX_TRACTION` / `0xA132FB5370554DB0`) — don't mix them up. This is what makes pre-race vehicle evaluation cheap — no car needs to exist yet.
- **XML supplier files**: the `<Model>` InnerText is the **cache key** — usually a signed-int hash for vanilla cars, but a string model name works too (for Add-On mods whose hash isn't yet recognized at native level, e.g. the unreleased Tiberius registers as `"tiberius"`). The `ModelName` attribute is the friendly model name, not the display name. Power/grip/top-speed are **not** in the XML — they come from the game at runtime via the model natives. The model-level natives take an **int model hash**, not a `Model` object — passing a `Model` crashes. Note: cars with a non-numeric `<Model>` skip the pace-cache (the int-hash natives won't resolve them) but the always-include path can still inject them directly.
- **Stat caches (`ModelGripCache`, `ModelTopSpeedMphCache`)**: `VehicleCatalog.BuildPowerCache` scans the `Vehicles\*.xml` pool (no natives on the background thread), then runs on the **main script thread** after loading and fills model → raw grip (G) + raw top speed (mph) via the model natives. **GTA natives must not be called on the background load thread (`Task.Run`) — that hard-crashes the game**; always do native-per-car reads on the main thread.
- **Pace index (`ModelPaceIndexCache` + `ARS.ComputePaceIndex`)**: a 0–1 pace proxy per model. Components normalized against **fixed absolute physical ranges** (not pool-relative min-max — the user explicitly wanted defined ranges so 0 and 1 are stable regardless of installed cars): top speed → `mph / 200` (0–200 mph), power → effective accel normalized against the effective ceiling. Weighted **0.75 top speed + 0.25 power**; grip is read but weighted 0 (ignored) for now. The ceilings are `TopSpeedMphCeiling` / `GripCeiling` / `AccelRawCeiling` consts and the weights are `PaceWeightTopSpeed` / `PaceWeightGrip` / `PaceWeightPower` statics. Power uses the electric-aware effective acceleration: `raw × (isElectric ? 1.67 : 1.0)` — ICE is the baseline (no multiplier), electric gets the first-gear difference (5.0 − 3.33 = 1.67) — normalized against `AccelEffectiveCeiling` (= `AccelRawCeiling × 1.67`). `ModelAccelCache` (raw G, ~0–0.5) and `ModelElectricCache` (from `GET_IS_VEHICLE_ELECTRIC` / `0xD839450756ED5A80`) are filled in `BuildPowerCache`. Braking is still deferred. `BuildPaceIndex` (second pass after the stat caches fill) computes and stores the index; `Racer` recomputes it from the same natives at spawn via the shared `ComputePaceIndex`, so the leaderboard number is identical to the selection metric. No arbitrary scaling divisor — raw native values against physical ceilings.
- **Selection** (`FillCachedCandidates` → `VehicleSelector.Select`): candidates are kept if their pace index falls within the user-selected pace bracket around the user-selected pace target; the qualified pool is shuffled and trimmed to the requested grid size. The old Disciplines tag filter is bypassed so the whole `Vehicles\` pool is considered. Watch the log ("Pace-matched candidates: N" and "BuildPaceIndex: N models indexed") to debug the band width. ## Per-racer state
- **Aggression** (0–100): grid-assigned (first=0 … last=100, rounded to 10); player stays 50. Scales avoidance extra buffer and allowed TCS wheelspin.
- **Pressure** (0–100): pure proximity × aggression — target ramps from the nearest rival within 100 m; rises slowly, falls quickly. It previously added a bidirectional `±3 m/s` offset to `followTrackSpd`, but that offset was removed so the speed pipeline is cleaner for testing. Pressure still drives divebomb/defend/arming and other tactical decisions.
- **Stability-aware grip (`AvgGroundStability`)**: a 0..1 per-racer multiplier on `CurrentMechanicalGrip` (`= Base × Ground × stability`). **Currently hardcoded to 1** — the previous wheels-off-ground detector (`WheelSlips` ≈ 0) was unreliable and triggered on decompression, so it has been removed. The field and the decrement/recovery loop are gone from `UpdatePerceivedGrip` until a trustworthy replacement is found. (The separate throttle-side `WheelsOnGround` 3 Hz check is unaffected — it cuts throttle on airborne, which is a different concern.)
- **Maneuvers as cards** (`Maneuver` on the racer): **all maneuvers are now hand cards** — no legacy arm blocks remain. Vocabulary: a card **plays** when it enters the slot, and **folds** when it ends — by its own fold condition or by the stale fold. Each tick with an empty `ActiveManeuver` slot, the hand is evaluated in priority order and the first playable card plays; a played card is never reconsidered mid-play (no unplaying — that kills thrash by definition). Hand priority: **ChillOut** (passive posture, evaluated first) → **DefendLane** → **DiveBomb** → **Yield**; **Nitro is slotless and resolves instantly** (burn timer + one-per-lap lap stamp are its only state), evaluated before the slot cards but never blocking them. Every slot card folds on its own condition, with the **8 s stale-armed fold** in `ConsiderManeuvers` (1 Hz cadence) as the universal backstop: DiveBomb and DefendLane fold when their armed apex is passed (DefendLane also folds when the chaser stops trailing — gone, despawned, or no longer classified Behind), ChillOut folds when the pack thins, Yield folds per-tick (fastest-responding fold in the system) when the target drifts beyond its exit distance. Nitro's burn is fixed-length by physics — no mid-burn abort. **Corner-commit lane (DiveBomb/DefendLane)**: the beside-the-rival inside line in `ComputeCornerTargetLane` holds for the card's whole life — it sits before the outside-approach hold check and ignores the hold latch, the release timer, and the `SuppressOutsideApproach` flag — bounded only by the lane method's own 5 s-to-apex window and the card's fold. Divebomb also draws a per-dive brake bonus (whole hundredths, appended to the learned factor for that dive only, never committed).
  - **ChillOut**: when the pack is dense (rival count within a crowd radius at/above the crowd threshold — race starts), halves throttle and pins intended speed to hold a standoff behind the closest rival ahead until the field thins. Crowd count reads the whole field via `ARS.Racers`, counting only cars **ahead on race progress**, and only arms above a minimum own speed — a slow car must not pin itself into a rolling roadblock, and the leader has nobody to crowd. Never arms in races with fewer rivals than the crowd threshold.
  - **DiveBomb**: fires at full throttle into a corner within its seconds gate, with at most one rival wall active (an open side to commit into — walls are the `ApplyRivalWalls` corridor; count tracked per frame in `ApplyRivalWalls`); target = closest rival we overlap or will reach within its reach-seconds window (`TimeToContact`). While active: inside lane beside the target, braking target deepened toward the corner (the dive's deeper target), and the effective brake factor = learned factor + a temp bonus (never committed; sampling pauses while any card is active).
  - **DefendLane**: covers the inside against a faster chaser that would reach the corner entrance no later than us. **Yield**: caps throttle and halves decel to let a faster, overlapping, higher-pressure rival by near the entrance.
  - **Nitro card situations**: contested (rival nearby and faster), defended (rival behind closing within the burn's reach), lonely (empty straight, slow, next apex far), finish spender (rival nearby, race almost over — skips the corner-entrance gate). Vetoes: braking, off-track, big steer angle, low gear on high-geared RWD cars (wheelspin — **AWD cars are exempt, detected via handling `fDriveBiasFront` at `0x48`: between 0 and 1 means all-wheel drive; offset anchored by the same struct math that puts steering lock at `0x80` and traction-lateral at `0x98`, both verified**), **fairness gate — AI nitro only arms when the player has nitro, unless the player isn't on the grid at all** (`ARS.PlayerParticipating`, set from the grid in `LoadGrid`, cleared in `CleanEverything`), and a circuit-wrap veto (the corner gate never fires inside the armed corner's span — the lap-wrap in `ForwardNodeDistance` otherwise made a just-behind entrance read a full lap away). Future idea: compute the true minimum fire gear from the known power multiplier vs wheel grip instead of the fixed gear veto.

- **Passengerize**: AI drivers shift to the passenger seat while a rival overlaps within combined half-length + 0.5 m, preventing contact swerves. Never applied to the player.
- Ghosting was removed during avoidance cleanup; rebuild later with the avoidance system.

**Duel model (designed via Council, not yet implemented).** The plan for making every card physics-aware instead of reacting to instantaneous rival speed. One shared primitive: predicted time-to-apex per rival, closed-form (~4 sqrt per rival, evaluated at the 1 Hz `ConsiderManeuvers` cadence — effectively free). Inputs come from each car's *own live plan*, not model-stat recomputation: rival `Racer` objects expose `NextApexSpeed` (already bakes in downforce + the per-car learned corner-speed offsets), `CurrentMechanicalGrip`, `Handling.BrakingAbility`, `Handling.EstimatedTopSpeed`, `Handling.Acceleration`, `Gravity`, and their own `ForwardNodeDistance` — all populated in `Initialize()` for every spawned racer. Decel is the exact `ApexBrakingSpeed` expression (`min(BrakingAbility×4, CurrentMechanicalGrip) × Gravity × _brakeDecelFactor`); extract it into one shared helper so the duel and the speed pipeline cannot drift apart. Model-hash natives (braking `0xDC53FD41B4ED944C` etc.) are only the fallback for the **player** (no AI plan exists for them) — and against the player require a wider margin (never dive the player on a coin-flip). Segment variant for straights: per-car `vEnd = min(topSpeed, √(v² + 2·a·L))` over the distance to the next corner entrance; nitro effectively multiplies my accel (calibrate the approximation in-game once).

Card upgrades this enables (all Council-endorsed):
- **DiveBomb gate**: arm only when the exchange completes — predicted arrival level-or-ahead at the braking target AND my apex speed carries the corner beside the target. Today the dive shortens the braking target with *our* decel and never validates against the rival's braking map; "I'm faster right now" conflates drag race with braking duel.
- **DefendLane threat check**: defend only when the chaser's own grip/braking predicts they genuinely pass (beat/match us to this apex). A chaser who merely out-drag-races but under-brakes is beaten by the normal outside hold + our own braking plan.
- **Yield corner-superiority**: arm only when the rival out-brakes OR out-corners us *for the specific upcoming corner*; a straight-line rocket that loses every corner gets no yield. Plus a race-progress-ahead gate (today Yield arms on any overlap rival, even behind on race progress).
- **Nitro segment advantage**: replace "rival faster right now" with "the burn converts a losing exchange into a pass completed *before* the entrance" (a pass completing inside the braking window is a dive-in-progress → veto). **Known bug to fix with it**: `rivalNearbyFaster` never filters `RelativePosition`, so a faster rival behind double-counts with the defended rule. Also gate "defended" on the chaser being straight-superior over the burn window.

Hazards / guard rails (Council consensus — respect all of these):
- **Self-consistency**: predictions must model the *post-commit* state on my side — a dive shortens its own braking target; Yield halves my decel — otherwise dives get green-lit that overshoot and Yield gets under-armed.
- **Line-radius correction**: the commit lane is ~half a track width inside; corner-phase comparisons need radius ± the lateral gap (decisive at hairpins, negligible at 200 m).
- **Slipstream is invisible** to the duel → defender predictions systematically under-estimate chasers; inflate chaser closing estimates or add a cheap tow term (chaser close behind + near-zero lateral → effective top-speed bonus; also improves nitro logic). Never resurrect the old 30 m artificial-traffic follow mistake when using long-horizon follow ideas.
- **Surface transients**: rival `CurrentMechanicalGrip` carries their one-tick `GroundGripMultiplier` (dirt/kerb); mitigate with margins, or use `BaseMechanicalGrip` + `DownforceGripBonus` for comparisons.
- **Latch verdicts** once per (apex, rival) like the existing arm-once approach pattern — two AIs running the same duel must not flip-flop at each other.
- **Mutual nitro**: the rival's `_nitrousLapUsed` is readable — include their possible burn in the prediction.
- **Margin floors**: declare a winner only beyond a tunable seconds margin; ties → conservative action. NaN-guard radii/speeds (Clamp(NaN) → min-bound gotcha).
- **Downforce is speed-dependent** — accept as uncertainty, demand margin rather than modeling it.

Sequencing (each step verified in-game before the next): 1) nitro segment-advantage + `RelativePosition` fix; 2) shared decel helper + time-to-apex helper + DiveBomb gate + DefendLane threat check; 3) Yield corner-superiority (+ self-consistency amendment); 4) extras later: "pick the fight where we're superior" playbook, dive-target selection ordered by duel margin instead of raw distance, tow term, yield exit on confirmed pass, duel line in the ShowInputs debug panel.

## Durable gotchas — do not "fix" these
- **Remap with a descending output range + `clamp=true` is inverted** by `Clamp(val, min, max)` when `min > max` (NaN also compares less-than-anything). Keep output clamps ascending — use a descending *input* range when you want a reversed map. (This bit us repeatedly.)
- **NaN discipline**: `Clamp(NaN, -limit, +limit)` returns the min bound → instant full-lock. Guard steering outputs and any clamp input that can be non-finite.
- **Sub-200 ms lag** acceptable; don't add plumbing for one-frame quirks.
- Synchronous setup/loading work can pause `OnTick` and temporarily suppress per-frame debug visuals; revisit those operations later if setup stalls need to become incremental.
- **Speed asymmetry** (above) is intentional.
- **`Handling.Downforce` reads the wrong handling field**. `VehicleMemory.GetDownforce` was reading offset `0x0014` from the handling struct, but the `m_fDownforceModifier` field in `CHandlingData` is at `0x0010` (the `0x0014` offset is `m_fPopUpLightRotation`). The "raw `258`" we saw for the panthere was actually `m_fPopUpLightRotation`, not downforce. The real `m_fDownforceModifier` for the panthere is `120`. Once the offset is corrected, `m_fDownforceModifier` is in the same units the engine's `CommandGetVehicleMaxTraction` formula uses, and the native's `13.42` decomposes cleanly as `m_fTractionCurveMax × (1 + 0.035 × m_fDownforceModifier) = 2.58 × (1 + 0.035 × 120) = 2.58 × 5.20 = 13.42`. **Correct principled grip divider** in `UpdatePerceivedGrip`: `grip /= 1 + 0.035 × Handling.Downforce` (recover `m_fTractionCurveMax`). The previous empirical `grip /= 4 + Downforce/100` hack was a curve-fit to the wrong offset's value range; remove it once the offset is fixed. See `E:\GTA\GTAVSP\GTAV Source\src\dev_ng\game\Vehicles\handlingMgr.h:874` (struct layout) and `script/commands_vehicle.cpp:6203` (`CommandGetVehicleMaxTraction` formula) for the source of truth. **The panthere verified end-to-end** (GRP `2.58`, DF `120` after offset fix + principled divider). Other cars with raw downforce `> 100` should be sampled for sanity before treating this as universally closed — the formula is correct, but it's only confirmed against one data point so far.
- **Offroad gravity bump requires a baseline reset before the multiplier.** `Racer.Initialize()` runs every race via `SetupRace` (and again on respawn paths), so without `Handling.Gravity = 9.8f` immediately before the offroad conditional, the `*= 1.2f` stacks multiplicatively across restarts — `GRV` drifts upward each race, eventually feeding inflated values into every speed/decel site. The reset line is load-bearing; do not remove it.

- **Two-projection route speed (future idea, 2026-09)**: the user rates the projection layer very highly — long-term, `followTrackSpd`'s geometric `√(g·grip·r)` could be replaced by a two-projection system: a *true* projection (ballistic `ProjectAhead`, self-calibrating via measured acceleration — no grip-model disagreement) and a *pessimistic* projection at reduced grip ("could I still hold this if the surface worsens?"). Design caveats the rebuild must solve: a projection is a **limiter, not a planner** — it assumes current velocity+accel continue, so it cannot see future braking decisions (the apex braking map stays for long-horizon work; projections cap what the plan may attempt); multi-second ballistic horizons are physically wrong; and a target derived from a projection that depends on current speed risks a self-referential feedback loop (needs damping or latched verdicts). The pessimistic variant's real value is anticipatory: ease speed *before* the edge instead of the current offshoot cap's after-the-fact emergency. Reference patterns: `ComputeOffshootSpeedCap`'s shape + the `_gripCheckLifted` handoff.
- **Off-track projection cap (`ComputeOffshootSpeedCap`)**: the projection layer's one live mechanism. When the 1 s ballistic projection (`ProjectAhead`) lands beyond the safe track bound **on the outside of a corner**, intended speed is capped toward a grip-scaled floor over an off-track-distance band — an instant clamp, no bias, no decay; in practice it only bites well past the edge (deep-off-track emergency, not a gentle band). It also lifts the grip checker (`_gripCheckLifted`, consumed in `RunGripChecker` to suppress corner-speed learning after an overshoot — live, do not remove). The old drifting ConfidenceMPS bias (projection-margin speed bias with lateral-load confidence gating) no longer exists in code; a rebuilt bias system should reuse the `ProjectAhead` primitive plus this method's shape and the `_gripCheckLifted` handoff as the reference pattern.
- **TRlat ladder — the slide-response envelope is parameterized by `Handling.LateralTractionCurve` alone.** Every slide-handling threshold is a fraction of it, handing off in sequence as a slide grows: ×0.2 = steer-limiter grip floor + brake-rampdown trigger (rampdown spans to ×0.4, i.e. 2× gripSteerAngle); ×0.5 = slide-countersteer *priority* blend start (final steer lerps toward "trajectory steering − countersteer correction" — the heading/recovery/side-by-side PD plus yaw damping with the countersteer correction applied, **lane pursuit dropped** — so a slide takes priority over the lane target without ever becoming the steer target itself); ×1.2 = slideScale ceiling (countersteer can reach 120% of slip) + priority saturation. Known band: between ×0.2 and ×0.5 the limiter already treats the slide as beyond grip but the countersteer is still legacy-only — if medium slides still steer inwards, start the priority ramp at ×0.2. The stored per-frame countersteer is `_slideCountersteerDegrees` (Racer), available for debug display.
- **Corrections are corrections — never reuse one as an absolute target.** A value stored as "subtract from the PD" (like `_slideCountersteerDegrees`) carries the *opposite* sign of the steer direction it wants; lerping the final steer toward it as a target steers **into** the slide. This exact mistake inverted the countersteer at full slide priority and was caught in-game 2026-09 (car sliding rightwards steered leftwards, off-track). When blending toward a slide-response target, reconstruct the full target (trajectory terms + correction applied), not the correction alone.
- **Steering limit** (`ApplySteerLimits`): **currently a slide-angle limit** — max steer = `Remap(speed_mph, rampSpeed→0, limitAngle → steer lock)`: full lock at standstill, collapsing to the limit angle by the ramp speed (both in code), applied equally for steering-in and countersteer. The limit angle is a small margin plus `max(|slide angle|, grip-based allowance)` (formula in code). The MaxBrake rampdown and MaxThrottle cut remain tied to the limiter. The old **speed-based reduction** (`steer /= 1 + 0.5 * fwdSpeed`, always-on, plus a TRLateral `×0.3` min-steer floor) is **zombie code** — preserved in a `/* ZOMBIE */` comment block inside `ApplySteerLimits` if this slide-angle approach is abandoned. **Zombie gotcha:** `Remap` with a descending *output* range + `clamp=true` inverts to a constant clamp (it returned steer-lock forever). Always use a descending *input* range so the output stays ascending.** The earlier **empirical peak-memory** system — it learns the steer angle that produced the best *measured* lateral G. The separate **speed-based steering reduction** (`steer /= 1 + factor * (fwdSpeed - threshold)`) and the **steering D term** (`steerKD`) are temporary feel-based carve-outs; both were lowered because the previous values felt overly restrictive/damped, but they are not grounded in measured tire behavior. **Everything is in Gs** (latG = `AverageAcceleration·right ÷ 9.8f`; `_bestLatG` stored in Gs; declared grip ceiling = `CurrentMechanicalGrip`, NOT × gravity — a lingering 9.8× unit bug lived here, never multiply grip by gravity and treat it as G). **The reference is now a linear proportional controller, not an exponential filter**: `ref += (Gs − ref) × gain × dt` (gain ≈ 5 → ~0.2 s time constant), symmetric rise/fall, no deadband — the input is spike-clamped before integrating (a collision impulse must not lift the reference even one frame's step). Utilization (current/ref) drives explore vs exploit: near the peak → exploit (limit = remembered steer + small margin, floored); below → explore (limit = `min(steerLock, bestSteer·1.5 + 2)`, floored — *bounded* per audit MAJOR-3, since full lock would map to ±1 input and make the clamp a no-op). Declared grip is only the spike-rejection sanity cap (`×1.1`). The two old limit systems (grip-saturation formula; slide-angle lerp) are commented out above. Hitting the limit caps `MaxThrottle` (pegged → 0.9, 10° past → 1/s cut). TRlat/declared curves are not trusted.
