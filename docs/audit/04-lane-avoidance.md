# AUDIT 04 — lane chain, rival walls, avoidance, rates and units

**Surface**: the lane override chain in `ComputeSteering` (`Racer.cs:692-728`), its five writers (`ComputeCornerLane`, `TryComputeCurveLean`, `ComputeAvoidAheadLane`, `TryPickAvoidanceSide`, `ApplyRivalWalls`/`ClampLaneToWalls`) and the single mover that carries the aim (`UpdateLaneMover`), plus every constant that multiplies `TickScale`.
**Date**: 2026-10-25.
**Build audited**: **958** (`build/build-number.txt` = `958`; source `HEAD 9bdb9af`, 31 commits ahead of `origin/master`). The live `Log.log` on this install was written by this binary today, 13:26-13:29, mid-race; its aggregated numbers are quoted below.
**Method**: read-only. No build, deploy, commit or game run. Two instruments: (a) a literal transcription of `ApplyRivalWalls` + `ClampLaneToWalls` stepped offline (kept in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\hermes\cache\scratch\wall_replay.py`, **not** in the tree), and (b) aggregation of the existing session log rather than reading it line by line.
**Verdict**: the mover is the sound part — one law, one place, every reason through it, and the driver-verified 26/24 pair survives inspection. The **wall state machine is not sound, and the reason is upstream of every constant that has been tuned in the last fourteen commits**: its geometry is a 500 ms snapshot consumed at core-tick rate, so targets step in 2 Hz chunks and no close/open rate can smooth them (W1). On top of that the ordering guard runs in a different space from the clamp that consumes it, and in the one band where that matters — three-wide, 3.3-5.2 m apart — the clamp throws both walls away silently (W2). Those two are blockers. The chain, the pass picker and the units are otherwise honest; the defects there are decisions and mislabels rather than broken laws.

## The override chain, as it actually runs

Last writer wins (`Racer.cs:703-726`), over a base of **the car's own lane**:

1. `steerRefOffset` — the car's lane, measured against its **own** current node (`:699`). This is the base and the fallback; zero is the centre lane, never "no answer".
2. **Curve lean** (`:716`) — gated on `!cornerLineActive`, and also overwritten by the corner below, so the gate's only real effect is that `_leanApproachMps` is not recomputed while the line is active (it is read only when the reason is `CurveLean`, `:1149`, so the gate is effectively cosmetic).
3. **Corner line** (`:717`) — applied only if `cornerLane != 0f`.
4. **Avoidance** (`:719`) — applied only if the pass lane is non-zero.
5. **Recovery edge** (`:720`) — only while `_stuckPhase == Drive` and the car is off the track.
6. **Rival walls** (`:721-723`).
7. **Debug pin** (`:724`), then the mover (`:727`).

Three things the order alone does not tell you, all verified above:

- **A zero is a sentinel, not a value** (`:717`, `:719`). Both the corner and the avoidance report "no opinion" as `0f`, so a demand that computes to exactly the centre lane is read as silence. Two of the three cases are latent rather than observed: the corner's offset passes through zero continuously and is unlikely to land on it in a float, and a pass lane is never zero in practice because its buffer is `rivalHalf + aggro` (≥ ~1.15 m). The **reachable** one is the lean: `Math.Max(laneEdge - LaneInsideGapMeters, 0f)` (`:1036`) returns zero on any track whose half width is within a metre of a car half, and the lean then reports "no answer" on every pass of that track instead of asking for the centre — the code's own sign guard manufactures the sentinel. None of the three is a crash; all three disappear if a source reports an explicit "has an opinion" instead of a magic zero.
- **A loser still acts** — through `_apexInsideNow` (L1), and through every latch the losing term wrote (`_linePhaseLatched`, `_lineClockMps`, `_lineBiasNode`, `_leanApproachMps`). The chain decides *this tick's request*; it does not decide what the mover is allowed to do with it.
- **The walls are not a ceiling on the chain, they are its third-largest author.** 301 of 1 400 logged mover lines name `WallClamp`, against 69 for `Avoidance`. Whatever the chain asks, one tick in five the wall is the thing that actually moved the lane.

## Not verifiable read-only

- **No build and no drive.** W1's staircase and W2's blackout are proven from the code and a literal transcription, not observed on track. The transcription is a copy of the two methods and was checked on the one case whose answer is fixed by construction: targets `(7.25, 6.0)` → mid `6.625` → guard `±1` → **5.625 / 7.625**, which are the figures `AGENTS/AGENTS-WIP.md:447-460` quotes from the pre-958 code. That confirms the guard rule and that `corridorHalf` is 1 m — it is also why D1(d) calls the WIP's "half a metre" wrong. The pre-958 shape was **not** re-run end to end, so those older numbers are reproduced as arithmetic, not as output.
- **W1's staleness interval is read, not measured.** 500 ms is `_rivalInfoTick = now + 500` (`:4544`) and the phase offset shifts it per car; the real interval is 500 ms ± one core tick.
- **The log aggregates are one session on one track** (13:26-13:29 today, build 958). They are evidence of prevalence, not of a rate: 55 `Lane guard` firings in a partial race is not a per-lap figure.
- **Whether the walls *should* read live geometry is an architecture question.** `UpdateRivals` is deliberately on the slow tick; making the wall's side and overlap live means computing two cross-car quantities per core tick, which is a cost decision, not a defect fix.

## Owed by the owner

**Decisions (no wheel time)**

1. **W1** — the fix is a choice of three: promote `overlaps`/`LateralGap`/`NodeOffset` to a per-core-tick read for the handful of rivals actually in the wall window; keep the slow record but gate the wall on `TimeToReach` alone (a time is robust to a gap error, in a way a 5.5 m overlap window is not); or accept the staircase and stop tuning rates against it. Nothing else in the wall block can converge until this is chosen.
2. **W2** — what a collapsed corridor should mean: clamp to the midpoint (the guard's own answer, in clamp space), keep the side that is nearer the car, or keep the narrower bound and let the mover's inertia do the rest. Discarding both is the one answer that is certainly wrong.
3. **Z1** — delete the dead hold (three constants, thirty lines, one driver verdict against it) or keep it as a documented switch alongside the other three. Keeping it as a `const` at least makes the compiler name it.
4. **L1** — whether the inside guard should be keyed on the reason (fire only while `laneReason == LaneReason.ApexLine`) or stay keyed on the phase. Keying it on the phase is what lets a corner that no longer owns the lane cancel an avoidance move; keying it on the reason re-opens the step it was built to remove.

**Drives** (one run, `Show Rivalry` on, one batch)

- **Three-wide at 4-5 m** (W2): the walls are drawn, the lane obeys nothing. If W2 is right, the car will move across both rivals' lanes without a `WallClamp` line.
- **A rival drifting across on a straight** (W4): the walls go out for about half a second and come back on the other side.
- **An edge-hugging rival** (W3): the wall asks for a lane outside the tarmac and the car stops at the white line anyway.
- **A dive up the inside at the apex** (L1): the lane will not open outward; only the contact push responds.

**Blockers**: W1 and W2 — the wall state machine's input clock and its collapsed-corridor branch. Neither is a crash; both make the walls a feature that reports one thing and does another.
