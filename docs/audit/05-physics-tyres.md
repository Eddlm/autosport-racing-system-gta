# AUDIT 05 — physics, tyre model, native writes and their restoration

**Surface**: the per-racer physics state (`Racer.cs` grip, slide, TCS, ABS and countersteer paths), every native write ARS makes to a vehicle or its driver, and the handling reads that feed them. The engine claims are checked against the leaked tree at `E:\GTA\GTAVSP\GTAV Source\src\dev_ng\game`.
**Date**: 2026-10-25.
**Build audited**: **958** (`build/build-number.txt` = `958`; `bin/Release/ARS.dll` and the live `Scripts\AutosportRacingSystem\ARS.dll` are one file, sha256 `12fb8b8ff31667a6…`; source `HEAD 9bdb9af`). The live `Log.log` is the session of 13:26-13:29 today: **14 AI cars, no player racer** (`Log.log:315` "Adding player to grid" is followed by an initialization block with no player car), so the player-side leaks below have no log evidence and are code/engine findings.
**Method**: read-only. No build, deploy, commit or game run. Three instruments: (a) the code and the leaked engine source; (b) the live `Log.log`, aggregated rather than read line by line; (c) arithmetic on the shipped constants and on the 14-car roster. The scratch arithmetic lives in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\hermes\cache\scratch`, not in the tree.
**Verdict**: the **handling layer is clean** — ARS never writes grip, downforce or a handling flag, so there is nothing to restore there, and the cheat-power write is reset by the engine every physics step. The defects are in three places: (1) **two native writes escape the race on the player's own ped and car** (proofs/health and the nitrous override); (2) **the apex plan is built from grip sampled at the car**, so every corner target inherits the current surface and the current corner's downforce; (3) the tyre-side laws are internally consistent but **read the authored pair without checking it**, and the roster is 9.2-13.9 °/G against the project's own 11 °/G reference.

Two things the table says. **The spread is 1.51×** (9.18 to 13.89 °/G), so "a slide" is a different absolute angle on every car. **The ceiling is proportional to the authored angle, not the grip**: the grippiest car (BUFFALO3, 2.45 G) gets the **tightest** ceiling relative to the reference (1.11×), and the least grippy (DOMINATO, 1.8 G) the most generous (1.68×). If the 11°/G line is right, the low-grip/high-angle cars are allowed past their peak and the high-grip/low-angle cars are held under it — the opposite of what a stability ceiling should do. The band has the same error: DOMINATO starts 26 % late, BUFFALO3 17 % early.

This is not a claim that the authored angles are wrong — the meta is the meta. It is that **ARS has no consistency check and its laws are not grip-scaled**, so an off-line car is driven by a band and a ceiling calibrated to a tyre it does not have. The fix is a decision (P6 in "Owed by the owner"): report the ratio and refuse/repair the pair, or scale the band and ceiling by the live grip rather than the authored angle.

## The ABS taper — dead, and the two statuses behind it

**No part of the ABS taper is live through another path.** `AbsEnabled` is `false` at `AutosportRacingSystem.cs:172`, forced `false` at load (`:3508`), and the menu row is unchecked and `Enabled = false` (`:1086-1092`). `AbsCapLevel` returns `1f` on the first line (`Racer.cs:2837`); `Control.MaxBrakeFromABS` is assigned that 1 every tick (`:2818`), and `ComposeBrakeCap` only names `BrakeReason.Abs` when the cap is **below** the base brake (`:2891-2899`), which a 1 can never be; the projection brake reads the same 1 (`:2140`). `BrakeLockSlip`, `AnyWheelRotationBelow` and `MinWheelRotation` have no caller outside the ABS body and the ABS log line (`:2823-2833`). The grep is the proof: `grep -n "AbsEnabled\|AbsCapLevel\|BrakeLockSlip" src/*.cs` returns the flag, the menu, the loader, `AbsCapLevel` and the log — no second consumer.

The **settings default is the only live remnant**: `SettingsRepair.cs:150` declares `AbsEnabled` default `"True"`, so a fresh install writes `True` into the menu ini while the loader ignores it. That is already **AUDIT 10 F4**; it is repeated here only because the physics audit is where a reader will look for it.

Why it is off is correct, and the engine confirms it. `Automobile.cpp:3769-3775` re-grants `WF_ABS` to every `STATUS_PHYSICS` car every tick, and `wheel.cpp:5206-5216` forces the published slip ratio `+0x174` to `±sfAbsActiveSlipRatio` (±5) every fourth frame while `WF_ABS_ACTIVE`; the engine's own quiet clamp is at `1.5 × 0.4 = 0.6` of slip, so the taper's 0.5 knee has 0.1 of slip to work in before the engine's clamp. On the **player's** car (`STATUS_PLAYER`), the grant only comes from the handling/model flags, so the taper would be the only ABS there — a different law on the two sides. `AGENTS/AGENTS-VEHICLE-STATUS.md:11` states the grant; the code comment at `Racer.cs:1086-1087` says the same.

⚠️ **Doc conflict**: `AGENTS/AGENTS-VANILLA-STEERING.md:142` says the "every AI car gets ABS" effect is **retired — flag-less AI cars genuinely lock up (driver-observed)**. The engine path above and `AGENTS/AGENTS-VEHICLE-STATUS.md:11` say every `STATUS_PHYSICS` car re-earns it every tick. The driver observation may have been a **lateral** slide (the ABS clamp is longitudinal) rather than a locked wheel, but as written the two project docs disagree; the code chose the engine's reading, which is the right call. This is a documentation fix, not a code one.

## Countersteer, band and damper — units, composition, full lock

**Units are honest.** `SlideAngle`, `Control.SteerDegrees`, `CountersteerSlideShare` (dimensionless), the assist ceiling (a share of the slide in degrees) and `CountersteerAssistDegreesPerSecond × TickScale` (deg/s × s) are all consistent (`Racer.cs:1754-1775`, `:871-874`). The damper gain is `SteerDampingGain / max(BaseMechanicalGrip, 1)` in **seconds** applied to a yaw error in deg/s (`:1793-1798`), so the term is degrees; the divider is a real per-car grip normalisation, not a unit conversion.

**Composition, in order** (`ComputeSteering`): `nonDamper = sideBySide + lane` (`:825`); the damper subtracts and may cross neutral by up to `DamperCountersteerMaxDeg` 4° (`:826-843`); the slide blend lerps the command toward `sideBySide − 0.5×slide` by `_slidePriority` (`:845-855`); the earned extra subtracts up to the remaining `0.5×slide` at 10°/s (`:858-874`); the contact term is added after (`:880-881`); the limiter clamps last (`ApplySteerLimits`, `:1928-1975`). The total at the top of the band is `sideBySide − slide`, i.e. the wheel points along the velocity vector **when the pursuit command is zero** — with a non-zero `sideBySide` it is that many degrees short of neutral, which the "neutral point" comment does not say.

**The band is 1.0 to 2.5 × the live peak**, `TRLateralAtSpeed`, not the authored zero-speed angle (`:818`); the damper's gate is the one law that still uses the authored value (P8). **At full lock**, the limiter's countersteer allowance is `min(|slide| × _slidePriority, SteeringLock)` on the side the request is on (`:1953-1958`), and the damper's own bypass opens the side to `SteeringLock` when the term opposes the yaw (`:1962-1970`). If the slide exceeds the lock, the wheel is clamped at the lock and cannot reach the neutral point; that is physical, but it means `CountersteerTotalShare = 1.0` is a statement about the **command** and not about the wheel once `|slide| > lock`.

**The two composition defects** are P9 (the extra is not weighted by the band) and P8 (the damper gate's reference). **The pedal handover** is a cap, not a cut: `CountersteerPedalCap = 0.5` on both pedals while `IsFullCountersteer` (`:2112-2116`), and the plan keeps its direction under the cap; the other reason caps still compose on top (`:2851-2885`, `:2889-2899`). That is the shape the notes ask for.

## Player versus AI — which car the physics runs on

The **whole tyre stack is AI-only**. `ProcessAI`'s `!ControlledByPlayer` branch runs `ComputeTargetSpeed`, `ComputeSteering`, `UpdateThrottleReasonCaps`, `UpdateBrakeReasonCaps`, `ConvertSpeedToPedals`, `ApplySteerLimits` and `TranslateSteerToInput`; the player's branch runs only `UpdateNitrous()` (`Racer.cs:4543-4574`). So the slide band, countersteer, damper, steer ceiling, TCS and ABS are **not applied to the player's car at all**; the only ARS physics on it are the overspeed drive-force cut (`UpdatePowerCompensation`, `:3757-3764`) and nitro. This is by design — the player drives — but it means every tune of the slide band/TCS/ABS is an AI-only tune, and the player's car cannot be used to validate it.

The **status split** is the second half. An AI car is `STATUS_PHYSICS`, re-granted `WF_ABS` every tick (`Automobile.cpp:3775`), and its `+0x174` is forced to ±5 while the engine ABS is active (`wheel.cpp:5206-5216`); the player's car is `STATUS_PLAYER` and has no such clamp unless its flags grant ABS (`AGENTS/AGENTS-VEHICLE-STATUS.md:11`). So the TCS and any future ABS law read **different data** on the two sides, and a threshold validated on an AI car is not the same threshold on the player's. The **unintended crossovers** are P1 (the ped proofs, because the driver block is not gated) and P2 (the nitro override, because the player's car is the one that is kept); the AI-only car natives leak only through P14.

**Rocket boost** is the block that looks shared but is not: it sits outside the `!ControlledByPlayer` gate (`:4509-4512`), yet its enable uses `Control.Throttle > 0.9` and its disable uses `Control.Brake > 0.1`, and the player's branch never writes either field. It is inert for the player and can only latch on a kept AI car (P15).

## What is clean, and why

- **No handling writes.** Grip, downforce, handling flags, ABS/TCS bits: all read-only. `VehicleMemory` has four setters, all on the control struct (`VehicleMemory.cs:19-22`), and no other file writes a vehicle field (`grep MemoryAddress` → reads only). There is nothing to restore, and a bad race cannot leave a car with altered grip.
- **The cheat-power write is engine-reset.** `CVehicle::ProcessPostPhysics` calls `SetCheatPowerIncrease(1.0f)` every physics step (`Vehicle.cpp:2186-2188`), so the once-per-frame writer at `Racer.cs:3787` cannot leak between frames. `Transmission.cpp:1364`, `:2318` consume it on the drive force only.
- **The control struct is AI-only and per-frame.** The player's car is never written; the AI cars are deleted. The only control writes on a surviving car are none.
- **The ABS taper has no live path.** The static, the loader, the menu and `AbsCapLevel` all close it; the settings default mismatch is AUDIT 10 F4.
- **The countersteer units are consistent** and the limiter is last, after every writer (`Racer.cs:1928`, `:4563`), which is the pipeline order the notes require.
- **The live `ArithmeticException` in `ComputeSteering` is inherited, not re-diagnosed.** AUDIT 08 B1 owns it: a NaN `SlideAngle` reaches `Math.Sign` at `Racer.cs:864`/`:874` and throws out of the tick, so the limiter and the pedal path are skipped on that pass. It is a runtime defect upstream of every steering law, and the fix there is the guard, not a tyre constant.

## Not verifiable read-only

- **No build and no drive.** P1/P2 are code+engine findings; the log is a **spectator** race (`Log.log:315`, no player racer), so the player-side leaks have no log evidence. P3's on-track size is arithmetic; P5's incidence needs a stop in the commit window; P11's exact-zero field is unmeasured; P6's spread is one session's 14-car roster and is installation-dependent.

## Owed by the owner

**Decisions (no wheel time)**

1. **P1** — restore the player's ped at teardown (`SET_ENTITY_PROOFS` false, max health/health back, critical hits on), or gate the driver block on `!ControlledByPlayer` the way the car block already is. The second is smaller and leaves the AI peds protected for their race; the first is the honest restore if a player ped can ever be an AI-protected driver.
2. **P2** — call `StopNitrous()` from `Racer.Delete()` (and from `CleanRacers`) for any racer with `_nitrousActiveUntil > 0`, and clear the override when `NitrousEnabled` goes false mid-burn. One line in each place, and the player's car stops keeping the system.
3. **P3** — the fix direction: keep a **mechanical** grip for the apex/braking plan (no current-corner downforce, no surface multiplier) and apply the downforce at the apex's own speed and radius, or freeze the apex speed at the queue build and declare the staleness. The route term may keep the live grip; the plan ahead may not.
4. **P4** — implement the engine's bit precedence (`0x100000` → 1.1, else `0x200000`/`0x800000` → 1.2, Mesa3 → 1.2) and decide whether the multiplier belongs in `Handling.Gravity` at all, or only in a wheel-integrator model. As shipped it is a 9.5 % corner-speed and 20 % braking advantage for flagged cars.
5. **P5** — zero `SlideAngle` below a small speed (1 m/s is the natural gate) or gate the brake learner and `KickFold` on `fwdSpeed`; the steering laws already are.
6. **P6** — either report the pair ratio and refuse/repair an out-of-line car at load, or scale the slide band and steer ceiling by the live grip instead of the authored angle. A 1.51× spread in the current roster is not a tuning problem; it is the law reading the wrong half of the pair.
7. **P7** — make the TCS read the **most negative** slip (`MinWheelSlip`) and leave `BrakeLockSlip` (the max signed) to the brake side; the two fields are already separate.
8. **P8** — use `TRLateralAtSpeed * SlidingFraction` in the damper gate, the same reference the blend uses.
9. **P9** — decide whether the earned extra is a second, unweighted share (then fix the comment and the "neutral point" claim) or is scaled by `_slidePriority` (then multiply it and the band becomes the only modulator again).
10. **P13** — fix the stale comments in the same edit; they describe a 0.5/0.8 band and a full-slide share that the constants no longer are.
