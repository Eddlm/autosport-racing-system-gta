**Agenda — the seven workstreams the driver named for the coming days** (no ladder tier yet; each needs scoping before it earns one)

- **A. Corner context, from the ground up** — entrance/exit landmarks are still wobbly and every consumer derives its own. The ask is ONE context-generation system naming the brake point, the turn-in and the apex, so the speed plan and the outside→inside switch read the same landmarks. **DIAGNOSED, analysis confirmed by independent review — and the premise needs correcting: the generator is DETERMINISTIC.** `GenerateRouteInfo` runs once per XML load and `BuildApexTable` only from it, every input a pure function of the file, so nothing oscillates within a session; the context is *arbitrary corner to corner*, not unstable. (If the on-screen numbers do move, it is the label switching held corner or a track reload, not the generator — the cheap test is to log apex/`StartNode`/`SupposedRadius` and lap the same track twice; the per-corner logger that already did exactly this was cut in build 472 as dead weight, so recover it from the commit before that rather than writing another.) The real defects, measured on 2-decimal rounded arcs at 1 m nodes: (1) `HoldsOneRadius` compares a 10-node run to **its own mean**, so the span walk passes at step 1 on almost everything (R=50/100/200 → 100% step 1; R=300 → 77% step 5; R=500 → 12% step 20) and `SpanMinimumMeters` therefore hands **every** corner a flat 20 m entrance and exit — a 200 m sweeper anchors its braking 22 m before the apex; (2) the apex is the argmin of the raw ±4-node circumradius, whose sagitta at R≥200 sits at or below the file's 0.01 m quantum (R=200 measures ~160, −20%; R=500 spreads 314–999), so the min-over-region bias costs ~11% apex speed and the apex can sit tens of metres off a sweeper's truth; (3) the chicane walk is `halfWidth*4` **nodes** (80 on a 20 m track) — longer than a 30° R=50 corner's whole 26 m arc — and a false positive grants +10 mph; (4) contexts are keyed by apex node, so an apex move silently resets the learned brake factor; (5) `entryRadius == exitRadius == 200`, so the `radius >= exitRadius` clause is dead and the only debounce is 4 relaxed nodes. **Fix, ranked**: apex from the *smoothed* series (a few lines, and it holds `SupposedRadius` so apex speed does not move — keeping the stability fix separate from the speed change that wants its own drive); then an absolute hysteretic span criterion with a floor scaled to the corner's arc length; then the dead clause and the chicane window in metres. **SHIPPED and driver-verified across every route**: the apex comes from the smoothed series, the span runs to where the radius opens (region-bounded), the aligned merge window is short enough that a U-turn's two apexes both survive — Figureight went from 6 corners to 8, and the driver's "they both survived" — and the survivor is chosen on the smoothed radius so it cannot flicker between loads. Not done: the chicane window is still a node count off the track width, and apex speed still reads the raw tightest radius.

Detection must not reuse the crest instrument (`GsAtProbeSpeed`), the live truth is `IsUnstable`'s ride-height read (`Racer.cs:1809`), and the speed plan is a radius envelope (`RouteIdealSpeedForRadius`, `Racer.cs:3239`), not a discrete brake point. **Not every jump is a hazard**: one that is straight, or that lands where no steering is needed, should be ignored, so ramps must be classified rather than treated alike. The discriminator is not the flight but the **input the car owes during it** — a required brake or steer between lip and landing point — which is the same shape as the crest, where the car goes light and must brake before the transition; the classification should fall out of the existing plan (`RouteIdealSpeedForRadius` over the lookahead plus the route curvature across the flight interval) rather than a threshold of its own.
42. `section-roughness-sweep` — **Lateral normal sweep: a section's stability (idea, future)** — sample the surface normals **across** the road at every station rather than only along its centre line. One cross-section then gives three things at once: the road's lateral shape (camber and bank) per station; a **roughness or section-quality figure** — with a population of normals a lone stone is an outlier rather than the only reading, which is the ray-cluster idea extended across the full width; and a check of the saved route against the real surface, since the route polyline is not the collision mesh. Cost is rays times stations inside the same load-time sweep that serves the material map and the ramp candidates, so it stays free per frame. Terminology: this is a property of the *track*, so do not call it stability — ARS already uses that word for the car's own state (`IsUnstable`, `Racer.cs:1809`); roughness or section quality says what it is. **Its motivation (driver):** some tracks bring the problem back where they leave the tarmac and return to the field - one zone is rough on the left while the right side is clean, and the rough side alternates along the track, and the cars are not aware of it. That makes the consumer **lane preference** rather than a report, and it makes the hazard **lane-dependent**: at the same station and speed a car on the clean side does not launch and a car on the rough side does, so being armed, being slowed and flying all depend on where the car is across the road. It also exposes a gap in the ramp design as recorded - the detection cluster is three rays at ±0.5 m on the **centre line**, a one-metre footprint, while the drivable width is up to about five metres a side, so a bump confined to one lane is invisible to it today.
**The classification, checked against the code (a teammate pass).** The brake half already exists and is not the speed envelope: `UpdateCornerRequirements` (`Racer.cs:2939-2980`) computes each corner's `brakingDistance = (speed² − intended²) / (2 · decel)` and its braking range, and deliberately does so over **all** corners rather than the held apex queue — so a bump is brake-hazardous exactly when some corner's braking span overlaps `[lip, landing]`. `RouteIdealSpeedForRadius` (`:3239`) is a pure grip-radius speed, the wrong tool for the aim question. The steer half is geometric and needs no invented tolerance: the car flies a straight chord while the route is an arc, so it lands off-line by the arc's sagitta (`ComputeRouteRadius` / `Circumradius3D`, `:3255-3280`) compared against the track's own `TrackHalfWidth` (`TrackLoader.cs:223`) — the tolerance is borrowed from the lane, not chosen. Marginal cases are **graded, not boolean** (`severity` in 0..1), for the reason `CrestDecelFactor`'s own comment gives (`:3178-3180`: a stepped decel cannot be integrated by sparse samples). The classification lives in the **consumer**, never the detector, because the flight interval is speed-dependent and the brake half is per-car, and because the detector must stay identical across the three modes or the A/B/C compares two systems rather than two behaviours. `AirOnly` ignores severity entirely — suppressing an input while the wheels are unloaded costs nothing — so only `SlowBefore` spends lap time on it. A bump on a crest takes `min` of the two speed effects, never their sum. **The flight is a fall, so its gravity is per-car**: `Handling.Gravity` (`DataStructures.cs:110`) is 9.8 scaled ×1.2 by the off-road flag trio (`Racer.cs:469-473`, the off-road bits `0x800000 | 0x200000`; the trio and the 39 cars carrying it are in `AGENTS-FLAGS.md:39-42`), so a car with the bit falls 20% faster, flies shorter and drifts less off its chord than the same car at the same speed without it. The ramp's published geometry therefore stays car-independent while the flight, the landing point and the classification are solved per car — one more reason the classification is consumer-side, and no cached landing node may be used as its input. Full design in the section below. Stage 0 - the altitude scan and its debug overlay - is built at `f3f318f`: compile-verified, not driven, and it changes no behaviour.

## Lane repulsion — unbounded on purpose, and that is the open question

**What it does**: for rivals inside a lateral and longitudinal gate box that are **actually closing laterally**, it *adds* a steer-away term to the lane steer — the code owns the curve; closing speed sets the magnitude and distance scales it, and a hard close at close range can dwarf the lane P. It sits **after** the P, so nothing the P does bounds it, and it is inside the lane component rather than a separate term.

**Why it was left alone**: it is the contact-avoidance term, and the lane P is now deliberately uncapped, so clamping the *sum* would weaken exactly the case that must not be weakened. It has always observed one rule — parallel traffic must not kill the lane steer — which is why it fires only on closing rivals and **adds** instead of overwriting.

**Open**: whether anything should bound it, and what: the whole lane component, the repulsion alone, or nothing, leaving the limiter as its only ceiling. Driving has not called it broken, so this is a decision rather than a defect — but if a car ever reads as swerving at nothing, this is the first term to look at.

## Downhill braking term — span fixed, one design question open

**Shipped (user verdict: "kinda works", then the same session confirmed the corner work overall)**: the corner braking solve now uses `BrakingDecel` = grip-limited base + the gravity component of the **mean grade over the braking span** (car → that apex's braking target, up to 8 samples across it). `TrackPoint.Elevation` is exactly `90·sin(pitch)`, so the slope sine is a direct conversion rather than a curve fit. Downhill subtracts from the decel, uphill adds; the result is floored positive because a steep enough descent would otherwise push it to zero/negative and the `√` would return NaN — which the pipeline reads as 999, i.e. *no braking at all*. The same helper now feeds the fallback `MaxSpeedForBrakingDistance`, which used to carry its own copy of the decel expression (including the Yield halving) — the drift risk is gone; the Yield halving lives inside the helper.

**Open when revisited**: (a) ~~the grade is averaged over the *unbuffered* span while the solve's `distance` is trimmed~~ — **fixed**: both call sites (`ApexBrakingSpeed` and the fallback `MaxSpeedForBrakingDistance`) now pass the trimmed `distance` to `BrakingDecel`, so the mean grade and the integrated distance cover the same zone; the old mismatch counted the coast to the apex in the mean (~10 m of grade at 40 m/s, often right where a corner crests or dips). (b) ~~the per-node decel *profile* is discarded~~ — **not a defect; do not "fix" it**: the relation is `v² = vApex² + 2∫a·ds`, so the **mean** of `a` over the span is the *exact* quantity and a steep patch early vs late genuinely cancels out. The real caveat is only that `BrakingGradeSine` *estimates* that mean from **≤8 evenly spaced samples** — more samples would sharpen the estimate, not change the concept. (c) brake learning is a **second feedback loop on the same physical effect**, though not the way this note used to say: `CommitBrakeLearning` counts *seconds held at full brake* against `BrakeFullTimeTarget` and scales that apex's factor — it never samples achieved decel — so a downhill apex gets a lower assumed decel from the grade term **and** drives its own factor down because it needed more full-brake time. **Still open (a design call, wants driving)**: whether that factor should be de-geared of the grade term so it keeps meaning brake efficiency/tyres, or left as a per-apex memory that legitimately encodes that apex's slope. (d) keep it distinct from the *lateral* hill-grip model (unsigned pitch lowering apex/route speed) — the two compose, they do not double-count, and conflating them during tuning would be a mistake.

## Pace is model-theoretical, not instance-measured (noted, not started)

**The pace index describes the catalogue model, not the car in front of you.** Every pace number in the system — the selection anchor, the player's pace in Relative mode, the leaderboard prefix — comes from *model-level* natives called on a model hash: `TryComputePlayerCarPaceIndex` checks `ModelPaceIndexCache` first (the catalogue cache) and only then falls back to model-hash natives, and `Racer.Initialize` recomputes `VehicleData.PowerScale` the same way. Nothing on that path reads the spawned vehicle or the handling struct, so **a stock model and a fully upgraded one score identically**.

Where the gap shows:
- **The player's own car.** A tuned player car is paced at its stock number, so the Relative anchor (and any `PaceTarget` picked to match it) describes a car that no longer exists — the field gets matched against a fiction. Note the cache is checked *before* anything else, so a cache hit on the model hash **precludes** any instance read by construction.
- **AI grid cars — no longer a gap.** This bullet used to be the other half of the problem: AI cars were built with upgrades (`AITuningLevel` 0–3, plus a supplier-XML `<Acceleration>` node driving the `EnginePowerMultiplier` loop in `ApplyAccelerationOverride`), so their real pace could exceed the number they were selected on. **All three are gone** — those paths died with the vehicle-XML teardown, and `RandomTuning` (the only other performance applier) was already unreachable before it was deleted. AI grid cars are stock models today, so the model-theoretical number describes them correctly and **the live gap is the player's car alone**.

**The machinery for the precise reading already exists** — this is the useful part. `Racer.Initialize` already reads *instance* data (`Handling.Grip` from the spawned-handle native `0xA132FB5370554DB0`, plus `Handling.EstimatedTopSpeed` and `Handling.Acceleration` out of the handling struct) to build `VehicleData.PerformanceIndex`, a separate integer index that no selection path uses. So the work isn't "invent instance reads"; it's "route the pace through the instance reads the Racer already performs".

**Two directions, in dependency order:**
1. *Precise player pace (independent, doable now)*: read the spawned instance — the spawned-handle natives are deliberately different from the model-level pair (native table in `AGENTS-TECHNOTES.md`) — plus upgrade state (engine/transmission/turbo/suspension mods, `EnginePowerMultiplier`) and the handling-struct grip. Fairness consequence to keep in view: the player's pace anchors the whole grid in Relative mode, so a more accurate player number changes the field the player races.
2. *Inferred pace for upgraded AI cars (do together with racer upgrades)*: once racer upgrades are a real system, pace must be a function of the **built** car — the applied upgrade set and tuning level feeding the same `ComputePaceIndex` — not the catalogue entry. Preserve the invariant that the leaderboard number equals the selection metric (both derive from `Racer`'s spawn-time recompute, so they must move together).

**Noted for later consideration (user request) — the discrepancy, and one idea on file.** *Discrepancy:* the grid's PI describes the **model**, so a tuned instance's real performance is not what the field was matched to — it only matters for the player's car today (AI cars are stock, see above). *Idea:* the upgrades **are** readable per instance, so "what the mods did to the power" is measurable rather than guessed — mod levels (`GET_VEHICLE_MOD`, `IS_TOGGLE_MOD_ON`, `GetModCount`, the turbo toggle) plus the handling-struct fields those mods mutate (drive force, brake force, traction, top speed; offsets in `VehicleMemory.cs`). Two uses: **(1)** read the player's spawned instance for a true anchor; **(2)** sample drive force/braking before and after each mod level *once, in game*, to build a per-level multiplier table — which would also let AI pace be **inferred from an upgrade set** without spawning anything, i.e. exactly what the design tension above needs. Not started; recorded so the idea isn't lost.

**The real design tension** (worth settling before either direction): reading the *stock* model is what makes pre-race evaluation free and spawn-free — the grid is chosen *before* any car exists. The moment pace needs an instance, AI selection either has to spawn-and-read (expensive, and structurally too late) or keep inferring from an upgrade set. That choice, not the native selection, is the decision to make when this is picked up.

## Brake learning: rise until it slides, as a ceiling (idea, not started)

**The idea (user, same session as the share rework in `1a43320`):** keep raising a corner's brake factor until a slide shows up, instead of aiming at a duty-cycle target.

**Why it is worth having at all — the share target is a proxy.** `BrakeFullFractionTarget` says how much of a braking phase may be spent at full pedal; it says nothing about whether that share is *safe at that corner*. A cold-tyred low-grip car can slide at 10 %, a grippy one may live at 60 %. The skid verdict is the only signal in the system that observes the real grip boundary rather than a stand-in for it.

**Why it belongs as a constraint, not as the controller.** The verdict is one bit per apex pass with a flat response (`BrakeSkidFactorStep` off the factor, whatever the depth), so driving the factor from it is bang-bang — rise, slide, cut, rise — a sawtooth whose amplitude is set by the up-step against the flat cut, i.e. the oscillation the old pedal-seconds loop had, now by construction. It also cannot tell "3 % too late" from "40 % too late", and it is noisy in the way a controller hates: the detector reads *body* slide past `TRLateralAtSpeed × BrakeSkidPeakMultiple`, which is also what a rival's nudge, a kerb strike, a deliberate off-line move or a bump produces.

**The form to build:** the share loop keeps aiming, and a slide sets a **per-corner ceiling** the rise may not pass — remember `factor − step` for that apex on the verdict, and let the ceiling creep back up slowly across clean passes so it re-probes as tyres, lines and traffic change. That converges to just under the observed boundary instead of dithering around it, and keeps the noise out of the steady state. The furniture already exists: `_brakeFactorsByApex` is that shape, and the verdict already runs at the right cadence (one per apex pass, deferred past the exit by `BrakeCommitDelaySeconds`).

**What it needs first:** *depth* — `_brakeCommitMaxSlideDeg` is kept, so record how far past the multiple the slide went and for how long instead of paying a flat 0.1; and a *cause filter*, because nothing today separates a slide the car caused from one it was given. One consequence to keep in view: with the slide as the operative ceiling, `BrakeMaxFactor` stops mattering for corners that never slide, and those would ride to the clamp — defensible in a fast sweeper, absurd in a gentle corner, so the ceiling has to be per corner, not per car.

## Slide brake rampdown — removed for the WIP, reimplement after v0.9 feedback

**What it was**: in `ApplySteerLimits`, the maximum brake used to ease with the slide angle — `Control.MaxBrake = slideAngle > gripSteerAngle ? Remap(slideAngle, gripSteerAngle*2, gripSteerAngle, 0.8, 1) : 1` with `gripSteerAngle = 2 + TRlat × 0.2`. Past that slide angle the brake cap fell to **0.8** (a 20 % cut) so the tyres could regain lateral grip; below it the cap was a plain 1.

**Why it is out**: it was tuned against the *old* steering allowance (`2 + slide` capped at `TRlat × 0.3`, ≈3.4° for the median car), which sat **below** its threshold (≈4.25° median). The allowance is now vanilla's speed curve as a ceiling — `lock / (1 + 0.075(v − 5))`, i.e. ~20° at 41 mph and ~15° at 60 mph on a 40° lock, far looser than the TRlat form it replaced — so the rampdown would engage **even deeper inside** the steering range: the car holding more steer while its brake is being cut, a pairing that was never tuned together. Rather than re-tune blind, it ships out and returns with feedback in hand.

**Its absence *is* "full throttle while sliding"**: with the rampdown gone the brake cap is 1 through any slide, and the only slide-pedal response left is the full-countersteer override — `IsFullCountersteer` now reads the blend saturated (`_slidePriority >= 1f`, `Racer.cs`), so it catches a spin rather than an ordinary slide. That coupling is why the symptom sharpened while the blend was being retuned; the pedal-side detail is in the overrotation section.

**When**: after the first WIP release's feedback — **v0.9 and over**.

**Reimplementing — the trap that made this more than a deletion**: `Control.MaxBrake` used to be *derived fresh each tick* — `Initialize()` seeded it once per race and `ApplySteerLimits` rewrote it every tick (1, or 0 under full countersteer). Deleting the rampdown outright therefore left the countersteer release with nothing to undo it, and the brake would have stayed dead for the rest of the race. The first fix kept a plain `MaxBrake = 1f` in the rampdown's place; that was then replaced by the better answer — the cap is now **stateful with a natural recovery, mirroring `MaxThrottle`** (`if (Control.MaxBrake < 1f) Control.MaxBrake += 2 × TickScale` in `ConvertSpeedToPedals`, sitting beside the identical throttle line). So the release is still instant, the restoration is a ~0.5 s ramp, and the value self-heals with no reset anywhere — including across `ApplySteerLimits`' early `return` on a NaN steer, which the per-tick reset used to skip entirely. Anything reimplemented here must respect that pair: **instant release, ramped recovery**.

## Overrotation — the pedal acts on rear grip (the wheel has a rotation term now too)

**Trigger**: the car rotates more than the corner wants, especially on exit. The user's steer: *"overrotation does not need to just be fixed by countersteering or a big D here, overrotation can be tweaked with throttle."*

**The steering path's old limits are history; what remains is the physics.** The old PID could not command countersteer until slip exceeded the aim-point chord angle (`β > c = ld / (2R)` — ~7° at 30 m/s in a 100 m corner), and above it its gain was only the pure-pursuit geometry (`2L/d`, a few tenths). **That PID is gone — tried, driven and abandoned, with the pre-PID chain restored** (`AGENTS-STEERING.md`) — and the restored chain answers over-rotation through the **slide-priority blend**, which reads `|SlideAngle|` directly at a much higher gain. What has *not* changed is the physics underneath: the wheel acts on rotation through the **front** tyres, so overrotation caused by the rear breaking away is still fundamentally a **rear-grip** problem, and the pedal remains the lever that acts on rear grip.

**The sign depends on which overrotation, and that is the whole hazard.**

- **Power oversteer** — the rear is saturated by drive force. Lifting hooks it back up. *Less* throttle.
- **Load-transfer / lift-off oversteer** — the rear is unloaded by forward weight transfer, or by a crest or kerb. Lifting makes it **worse**; keeping the throttle on holds the rear down. This is exactly why the code's full-countersteer branch already holds `CountersteerRollThrottle` — a hair of throttle so the tires keep rolling — rather than cutting.

A rotation → pedal law that cannot tell these apart will fix one and worsen the other. The discriminator is already in the code: **wheelspin**. TCS reads it; nothing new is needed to classify.

**What exists today.** `ApplyThrottleCap` / TCS is **wheelspin-driven, not rotation-driven**, so power oversteer is covered only insofar as it produces wheelspin — a high-grip car rotating without wheelspin gets nothing. The only rotation-driven pedal action sits at the **full-countersteer threshold** (`IsFullCountersteer` → `MaxBrake = 0`, then `CountersteerRollThrottle` in `ConvertSpeedToPedals`) — very late in the slide, and it is a *brake release* rather than a throttle authority. The brake-side equivalent is deliberately out for the WIP (item 23).

**The case that puts this on the map — full throttle while the car slides.** The comet's undrivability was finally traced here, not to the damper or the countersteer. Three things converge: the rampdown removal ships a brake cap of 1 through any slide (item 23); the only slide-pedal response left is the full-countersteer override, and its trigger moved to the blend's saturation — `IsFullCountersteer` reads `_slidePriority >= 1f` (`Racer.cs`), i.e. the 2.5× live-peak skid gate — so an ordinary slide gets nothing; and the wheel has deliberately given up the aggressive correction that used to cover for a wrong pedal, since the countersteer target answers only half the slide angle (`CountersteerSlideShare`, `Racer.cs`) and the damper's crossing past neutral keeps only half its authority (`DamperCrossingShare`, `Racer.cs`). If the pedal is the real lever, that is the argument for it: the wheel can no longer mask it. The blend, the shares and the slip-balance knee are **uncommitted work still being driven** — anchors are the code, not a commit.

**The shape worth trying later**: generalise the maintenance-throttle half from the full-countersteer threshold to a `|SlideAngle|` ramp, bounded so it never adds drive beyond maintenance. A slide-throttle law feeds speed — at a corner exit that is lap time, but it can also spin the rears, so it is a loop and needs the bound.

### Stability awareness — partially implemented
IsUnstable now reads on every timed core pass, never per frame, instead of a throttled wheels-on-ground latch: it fires when the chassis rides above the at-rest height captured in `Launch` or when its yaw rate demands more lateral acceleration than the known grip allows (`Racer.cs:1809`). ### Stuck recovery — one system, escalation included (`1769084`, revised `6c4e8e5`, escalation `ed7720c`)
One system, revised into a two-phase manoeuvre: a short straight reverse first (`StuckReverseMs`, wheels straight, `Racer.cs:3511`), then forward throttle steering at a point `StuckRouteAimMeters` **along the route direction ahead** of the nearest node (`Racer.cs:3522`) — reversing with the wheels turned swung the nose away from where the car was going, and the route-ahead aim keeps the steering convention unambiguous. One predicate is the verdict, `HasRegainedControl` (`Racer.cs:3350`): on the track, driving **forward** rather than merely moving, heading along `CurrentTrackPoint.Direction`, and not sliding past a fraction of the car's own traction curve, all **held** for a window rather than sampled once — and it both ends the recovery (`Racer.cs:3441`) and resets the attempt counter (`Racer.cs:3368`), so the counter no longer selects the steering phase. It runs until that verdict, and there is no countdown of failed windows: the escape has one **absolute budget** — six seconds from the moment it engages, never paused (`StuckRecoveryTimeMs`, `_stuckRecoveryEndTime` set once at `Racer.cs:3417`) — and the snap waits for the car to be under `StuckSnapMinSpeedMps` (`Racer.cs:3468`), so a car still moving is never interrupted. The budget being absolute is deliberate: a car that only slows at the end does **not** get a fresh six seconds, it is snapped on the first stop after the deadline. The cooldown is armed when the recovery *ends* (`FinishStuckRecovery`, `Racer.cs:3456`), not at the trigger — measured from the trigger it would be spent while the car was still reversing — and without it a car that had just retroceded re-triggered before it could build any speed, so it rocked in place instead of escaping; the cooldown itself is user-confirmed to help in game. **The off-track projection cap is speed-gated now** (`OffshootMinSpeedMph`, `Racer.cs:1324`), so below that gate an off-edge car is no longer handed a standing brake in the cooldown and detection windows. The old position-lerp-to-track escalation was removed — **but an escalation does exist**: it is that speed-gated snap, teleporting the racer to the nearest track edge with the same math as the player's menu `ResetToTrack()` (nearest `TrackPoint`, lane side from `SignedLaneOffset`, heading along the track, ~10 mph forward), then clearing its own stuck state. A P-controller on `MaxThrottleFromTCS` targeting an ideal wheelspin: a tame fixed target off-track, more permissive on-track as the slide angle grows (values in code), with the output clamped well above zero — it never cuts below a floor fraction of throttle. Slide response is purely proportional (countersteer = slideAngle × scale); there is no derivative term to catch the *rate of yaw acceleration*. A snap-oversteer situation spikes yaw faster than a proportional correction can track — a D-term or a yaw-rate change threshold would catch it before the car snaps around.

### Gravity vs grip & speed — SETTLED: two published values, one multiplier

**Decision (driver's verdict): the car publishes both, and neither is withheld — the offroad load rise simply lives in gravity only**, because gravity is the quantity the engine's flag actually raises (`m_fGravityForWheelIntegrator`). So the car knows what gravity it has and what effective grip it has, and no consumer loses either; what goes is the duplication. Concretely: delete the `handlingGrip *= gravityGs` bake in `UpdatePerceivedGrip`, keep the multiplier in `Handling.Gravity`, and make every site that hard-codes gravity read `Handling.Gravity` instead — gravity then reaches the speed and decel laws through the `√(μ·g·R)` and `μ·g` forms they already use, and grip stays the pure coefficient with surface and downforce still in it.

The bake fires only above 1 G (`gravityGs > 1`), so this changes offroad cars alone, and it is not a tidy: their apex speed falls from 1.20× to 1.095× a road car's and their braking decel from 1.44× to 1.20×, so the pedal plan and the corner speeds both move. It wants its own drive. Site to repoint: `AckermannCeilingDegrees` (`Racer.cs`).

### Nitro pacing — the charging rule
Every car is charged once at race launch and recharged on each lap increase (`Racer.NitroChargedLap` stamp, reset in `Initialize`; a mid-burn lap crossing retries until `IS_NITROUS_ACTIVE` clears). AI bottles are gated on `AiNitro != Never`; the player's own bottle is never gated; there is no free-roam refill. The charge target sits slightly above full so the below-full detection latch stays meaningful.

### Corrections are corrections — never reuse one as an absolute target
The rule is **now embodied in the code** (re-verified): `ComputeSteering` rebuilds the countersteer as a full target — `countersteerTarget = dampedCourseSteerDeg - VehicleData.SlideAngle` — and blends the final steer *toward that target*, never toward the bare correction term. `_slideCountersteerDegrees`, the identifier this note used to cite as the counter-example, no longer exists; the lesson is what survives it. A value stored as a correction ("subtract this from the PD") carries the **opposite sign** of the steer direction it wants. Lerping the final steer *toward* it as a target steers **into** the slide — caught in-game (car sliding rightwards steered leftwards). So when blending toward a slide-response target, reconstruct the full target (trajectory terms with the correction applied), never the bare correction value.

### Front slip angle is uncosted — bounded by the steer ceiling, never priced in the pedal
Both throttle-side traction responses are blind to it. `ARS.MaxWheelSlip` reads the per-wheel **longitudinal** value at `0x174` and TCS is driven by that, so a **rolling** front tyre at a large slip angle registers nothing; `VehicleData.SlideAngle` is the **body** slip (velocity vs nose), which stays small in pure understeer. So neither TCS nor the slide blend sees front slip at all, and the understeer loop (more cross-track error → more lane-P lock → more front slip → more error) is only bounded by the steer ceiling. Vanilla's AI-side equivalent is steering-based and **fades out below 10 m/s** (`AGENTS-VANILLA-STEERING.md`), so copying it would not have fixed the low-speed wash either — and what *did* address it was the **Ackermann ceiling**, which the driver confirmed. The honest read: front slip is currently **priced in the steering limit, not in the pedal**. If it returns, the order is (a) **measure it first** — `α_f ≈ δ − (bodySlip + yawRate × frontAxleArm / v)` uses state already held, so log it and plot a session before writing a rule — then (b) a `Min`-style throttle ceiling driven by it, never a decrement (see the tie-in lesson above), and only if the front is still washing.

## Apex speed reads the biased radius — the smoother radius is unused

`BuildApexTable` stores **two** radii per corner: `SupposedRadius`, the **minimum `PreciseCurveRadius`** over the region, and `DetectedRadius`, the **minimum `SmoothedRadius`** over the same region (`TrackLoader.cs:412`; smoothing is the harmonic mean of `1/radius` across a ±2-node window). Registration compares the *smoothed* reading, and the 50 m positioning gate reads `DetectedRadius` — but everything the car actually drives to reads `SupposedRadius`: the stored apex speed, `NextApexRadius`, `ApexSpeedWithDownforce`, and the `intended` speed the requirement flags compare against.

**Why the gap matters.** A minimum over many noisy samples of an 8 m-chord circumradius is the low tail of that noise, and apex speed goes as `√(grip·g·R)`, so a radius biased low holds **every** corner speed down — the AI brakes more than the arc calls for. `DetectedRadius` is the steadier number, which is exactly why registration trusts it; so the two halves of the system disagree by construction.

**The change to make.** Move the read sites onto `DetectedRadius` — or store the smoothed value into `SupposedRadius` at build time and keep one field, which also stops the debug label needing two numbers. It **raises corner speeds everywhere**, so it wants its own drive rather than riding along with another change. The circle label already prints both as `R plan/detected`, which is how to see the current per-corner gap.

**Deliberately not done yet**: the user asked for this to be noted for a deeper look later rather than changed in the corner-requirements session.

A third reading now exists beside those two: `ExactRadius`, the tightest chord of the three, computed with them (`TrackLoader.cs:214`) and read only by the corner span walk — so which radius a consumer should trust is a three-way question now, not a two-way one.

## A constant-radius track generator undercuts the span rule

`GenerateArc` (`AutosportRacingSystem.TrackCreator.cs:253`) gives every creator section a single radius, tangent-continuous at the joints. The span rule leans on the opposite: it walks outward from an apex until the measured radius holds steady, which works on a converted route because the radius is unsettled around a real apex — an apex is the region's *tightest* node, so it is a local minimum with the radius rising away from it.

On a constant-radius section that premise weakens. The apex is still the region's measured minimum, but it is only a noise dip, and the true radius does not change away from it — so a stable run can begin immediately beside the apex and collapse the span. Since the entrance *is* the braking target, a collapsed entrance leaves the plan almost no distance to brake over, which is a worse failure than the long spans this rule was written to remove.

The likely mitigating factor: `SaveRoute` rounds coordinates to two decimals and that hits a short chord hard, so the noise floor on a created track may be what keeps the walk honest. **Unverified either way** — nobody has driven a creator-made track under the span rule, so watch created tracks specifically. If a span ever collapses there, the lever is the stability tolerance and the window beside it (`TrackLoader.cs:435-436`), not the shape of the rule.

## Route read baselines — one ladder, and it belongs in metres

**The proposal (driver's): fix a smoothing standard — three nodes, four nodes — instead of letting each read choose its own.** The instinct is right: the reads carry an unstated ladder today (`Direction` ±2, `Elevation` ±3, `ExactRadius` ±2, `PreciseCurveRadius` ±4, `GeneralCurveRadius` ±10, the crest scan ±6, and the live crest grip geometry-scaled) with no convention saying which rung a new read should take.

**A single number would be wrong, and the reason is the tension already recorded for the crest**: localisation wants a short baseline, because a long one shifts where a feature starts, while amplitude and severity want a long one, because on rounded coordinates a short read is mostly noise. The standard is therefore **two named rungs with a stated purpose** — a short one for location, a long one for strength — plus the rule that a new read reuses a rung and says which, rather than inventing a third.

**And the rungs cannot be counted in nodes**, because a node is not a metre: measured spacing runs 0.88–2.39 m/node, so ±3 nodes is about 2.6 m on one track and 7.2 m on another, and the same standard would be a different filter per track. The rungs belong in metres, converted with the track's own spacing — the same conclusion the crest window reached.

**Where it lives**: per the code-first filter the convention belongs in the code — a small named set of baselines and the converter, so a read cannot silently invent one — while memory keeps only the why (two rungs, in metres, and which purpose takes which). Awaiting the driver's call on the metre-based form.
## Crest move gating — the objective is set, the constants wait on a measurement

**The objective (driver's words): the corner must account for the crest so the cars know to brake earlier, because on arrival they may be in the air.** That is what the gate has to serve, and it says what the move is for: the braking plan starts up on the crest top so the car spends its loaded distance braking before the unload takes the wheels — an airborne car has no brake to apply.

**What the objective implies for the gate.** A fixed node cap answers the wrong question. What matters is whether the crest falls inside the **braking span** and whether its unload is strong enough to take the braking away; the braking span is speed-dependent, so one fixed count under-serves a fast approach (the car brakes over a longer distance, and a crest further back is still reached while braking) and over-serves a slow one. The gate should test that: move the entrance when the crest sits within the braking distance for the speeds this corner is approached at and its unload clears the severity floor; otherwise leave it, because the live vertical-load cut still takes speed off. **When in doubt, err generous** — too generous costs lap time, too tight risks the airborne arrival the task exists to prevent.

**Still open: the two constants**, and they wait on the instrumented run — the threshold because its evidence is censored, the window because the noise floor has never been measured.

**What "flat" means here (driver's clarification): constant grade, not level ground.** A straight climb counts as flat, and it stops being flat when the track begins to change its grade — the onset of the vertical curvature — so the trigger is curvature, never `grade ≈ 0`. That is already what the scan measures: `HillGripDeltaGs` reads three points either side of a node and returns speed-scaled `v²κ/g`, so the run's outer boundary **is** the curvature onset and the negative sign is the unload. It also means the entry point moves with the threshold, since the boundary is where `|κ|` crosses it — so the threshold is not a cosmetic constant, it sets where the braking starts.

**Where the entrance moves to — corrected: the crest's ENTRY, not its peak.** The right moment is where the track stops being flat and starts to curve downward, the onset of the negative vertical curvature, not the top of the unload. Braking at the peak spends the ramp already unloaded; starting at the entry is the last point at which the car is still fully loaded, and that is what "brake earlier than an airborne arrival allows" needs. The scan already walks exactly that run but keeps only its most negative node and its length (`AssignCrestNodes`), so the entry node has to be recorded as it goes — in that backward walk the **last node added before the run ends** is the one the car meets first. Two consequences: the move gets longer, so the node cap binds more often (err generous, above), and `corner.StartNode` is also the node the corner line, the release and the learned brake-factor sampler read, so all of them shift with it. It wants its own drive.

**The window is too narrow, and the live path already proves the fix.** The generation measure uses a fixed ±6 nodes (`GsAtProbeSpeed`, `TrackLoader.cs:489`) — a 12 m baseline — while the live crest-grip measure takes its outer samples from the corner's own entrance and exit, a geometry-scaled baseline (`CrestGripSpeedFactor`, `Racer.cs:1683`). On 2-decimal rounded coordinates a wider baseline is strictly better for the estimate: the sagitta grows with the square of the baseline while the rounding floor does not, so the curvature's signal-to-noise rises with it — exactly what noisy old tracks need. Two couplings to respect: the **onset** still wants the narrow window, because a wide one smears where the crest starts and the entry node is now the target, so strength reads wide and location reads narrow; and the threshold cannot keep its value when the window changes, since it is compared against the number the window produces. Root cause worth naming: the noise is `SaveRoute` writing 2 decimals — smoothing is the fix for tracks already saved, precision is the fix for new ones (an existing open item, and it rewrites every saved file).

**Smoothing trades noise for information, and here that trade is asymmetric.** The rounding noise is bounded and small, so it manufactures *mild* false crests; smoothing can erase a *genuine short, sharp* crest, and the sharp ones are the dangerous ones — the failure mode is the airborne arrival this task exists to prevent. So the resolution is not to choose a scale: **read strength wide and onset narrow, and when the two disagree keep the more conservative** — a peak the narrow scale finds survives even if the wide estimate averages it away.

**Set the window from the noise floor, not by taste.** Three points spaced w metres apart give a Gs whose rounding noise is roughly `1.2/w²` G at the 40 m/s probe (0.01 m quantisation, uniform): ±3 nodes would sit at the threshold, ±6 at about a fifth of it, ±12 at a twentieth. That is why a wider baseline is not merely quieter — it is what lets the threshold *come down*, which is the only way the censored log ever shows the mild crests it hides today. Caveat: that is a uniform-rounding estimate, so the instrumented run should measure the real floor rather than trust it.

**And the window is counted in nodes, which makes the baseline track-dependent.** ±6 nodes is about 5.4 m on a densely sampled track and 14 m on a coarse one (measured spacing runs 0.88–2.39 m/node), so the same window is a different physical baseline, a different noise floor and a non-comparable Gs across tracks. The window belongs in metres.
**Guard against smoothing stacked on smoothing (driver's warning).** The vertical is already filtered at generation: `TrackPoint.Elevation` is a ±3-node central difference of the route nodes (`TrackLoader.cs:207`) — a grade, not a raw elevation — and `Direction` is a ±2-node read beside it. The crest path does **not** stack on that: `HillGripDeltaGs` takes the raw `Position` Z and fits the profile over ±6 nodes, so the unload is a second difference over twelve nodes, independent of `Elevation`. **The trap is how easy that is to break**: building the curvature by differencing `Elevation` would difference an already-differenced quantity, compounding the filter and shifting the onset — which is now the target node. So curvature always from `Position`, baseline always stated in metres, never from `Elevation`; the horizontal side already keeps that discipline explicitly, with a baseline ladder (`ExactRadius` ±2, `PreciseCurveRadius` ±4, `GeneralCurveRadius` ±10) and the apex table reading the smoothed rung. **One inconsistency to reconcile**: the speed solve's grade samples `Elevation` (±3) while the crest's unload reads raw Z over ±6, so "grade" and "unload" describe the same stretch of road through two different filters.
**What the code does today**: `AssignCrestNodes` (`TrackLoader.cs`) treats a crest as real only past a curvature threshold measured at a fixed probe speed, and moves an entrance onto one only within a distance cap — the two constants this section is re-deriving.

**The magnitude idea is falsified, not merely parked.** It assumed a road/offroad separation — move for gentle crests, leave the violent ones to the decel term as terrain bumps. Logged crests break it: a road track (`Chianski Long`) produced a crest stronger than Offroad Hardcore's *floor*, so there is no surface correlation to separate on. Surface is not a defensible alternative either: it is not in the track file or in `TrackPoint`/`CornerPoint`, and the only path is `GetSurfaceHash` (`AutosportRacingSystem.cs`), a dead native world raycast with no road/dirt material hashes established anywhere.

**Why the threshold's own evidence is censored.** The scan *stops* at the threshold, so every logged `CrestGs` is above it by construction and the log cannot show how many genuine crests sit just below. The weakest crests observed sit within roughly a fifth of the threshold, so "real crests live in an empty band above the noise floor" is not something the current log can support — and that empty band was the main argument for holding the threshold where it is. Cheap test if it is ever worth revisiting: one diagnostic load with the threshold dropped, or log the unthresholded minimum per scan window. Note the live crest discount in `ComputeTargetSpeed` applies with no threshold at all, so a missed crest still gets its speed cut — only the early move is lost.

**Not a bug, but a reader trap**: the corner requirement horizon uses the grade-free `BrakingDecelBase` while the braking plan now uses the crested `BrakingDecel`, so the two no longer compute the same braking distance. It errs safe because the entrance move hands distance back at the same time, but they will read as agreeing when they do not.

## Launch model — designed, not built (parked)

Start at a corner entrance with the car at a multiple of its intended speed and integrate a ballistic projection forward, metre by metre, so a crest that unloads the tyres past gravity shows up as an airborne run. A crest launches exactly when `v²κ/g >= 1`, which is the crest's own stored Gs at that speed, so no third curvature scan is needed — the ballistic half only measures how far the car stays off the road.

Settled shape: read-time, because grip and the offroad gravity multiplier belong to the car rather than the table; walk **backward** from the entrance to find candidate launch nodes and **forward** from each to fly it; the parabola in closed form, `z(s) = z0 + tan(pitch)·s − g·s²/(2v²)`, sampled per node; pure point mass with no damping, since the fleet's suspension is unmodelled and any damping constant would be invented. Gate on **both** distance (car length as the opener) and **time**, because a fixed distance is speed-dependent. Two tiers: at the car's real speed, floor the braking decel across a predicted airborne stretch, as an airborne car cannot brake at all; at a doubled speed, treat the launch as a severity flag and a veto on moving an entrance onto it.

**Why it is parked**: on the smoothest road track nothing launches at 1x or 1.5x, and only two corners launch at 2x — because the crest work already walks the entrance back and hands the car its braking distance. Revisit only if a car is seen genuinely airborne into a corner. The `2x` probe is not a filter on its own: at twice the corner speed the test reduces to `vertical radius < 4 × corner radius`, which flags the smoothest track in the game.



## Bumps and ramps - the airborne window (item 41)

**Detector.** The crest instrument is blind to a bump: `GsAtProbeSpeed` is a fixed ±6-node window (`TrackLoader.cs:491-498`) and `HillGripDeltaGs` a three-point second difference (`AutosportRacingSystem.cs:2522-2556`), so a short lip averages to zero. Read the **raw `TrackPoint.Position.Z`**, never `TrackPoint.Elevation`, which is already a ±3-node difference (`TrackLoader.cs:207`). Instrument on **grade, not curvature**: a two-metre rung measures the departure tangent and a three-metre rung the onset, a lip is a **convex break** - a rise within `BumpMaxRunMeters` whose grade then flattens or drops - and the departure rung stays short so it never spans past it; longer runs are crests. `AssignBumpNodes` beside `AssignCrestNodes` (`TrackLoader.cs:359`), `ARS.Bumps` beside `Corners`, and a `Bump { LipNode, RiseStartNode, DepartureGrade, LipCurvature }` - **geometry only, car-independent**. *(Both the footprint and this scalar shape are superseded by the lateral bands at the end of this section.)*

**Ramp model, per car.** The car leaves the lip when `v²·κ_lip / g` reaches 1, then flies `z(s) = Z_lip + tanθ·s - ½·g·(s/v_x)²` walked node by node; the landing node is where it meets the road again, and `peakHeight` must clear a floor before anything arms. **`g` is the car's own `Handling.Gravity`** (`DataStructures.cs:110`, set at `Racer.cs:469-473` where the off-road flag bits scale it), so a flagged car launches less readily, flies about 17% shorter and lands about 31% less off-line. Carry no 9.8 into the ballistics, and if a car's gravity is ever unavailable fall back to the **higher** value, because under-predicting is the dangerous direction.

**Two corrections to the brief.** A bump does **not** always force a jump - at low speed the car stays grounded - so the window is predicated on the computed launch with the live truth as veto. And the ramp model is not a different law from the g-spike: both are the launch test above (`AutosportRacingSystem.cs:2552`). The ramp wins because a grade is legible at a short baseline, it yields a duration and a landing point, and it makes the per-car gravity explicit.

**Modes and the window.** `BumpBehaviour { Off, AirOnly, SlowBefore }`, default **Off** as the honest control. The detector, the flight prediction and the window stay *identical* across the three, or the comparison measures two systems rather than two behaviours. The window opens only on the ride-height clause of `IsUnstable` (`Racer.cs:1811`) and closes after a few grounded cores; **the prediction never opens it**, and `IsUnstable` itself must not be reused, because its yaw-demand clause (`:1815-1816`) would freeze the steering of a grounded car that is merely rotating. `AirOnly` freezes the applied steer and zeroes the brake while genuinely airborne and touches nothing else; `SlowBefore` adds a cap-only approach change in the clipped-ramp shape `CrestDecelFactor` uses (`Racer.cs:3181-3203`) plus a bias toward straight before the lip.

**The classification** (from the earlier pass): hazard is an input owed inside the flight; `severity = clamp(max(offLine / drivableHalfWidth, brakeOverlapLength / flightLength), 0, 1)`. The brake half reuses the queue-independent `brakingDistance` / `inBrakingRange` (`Racer.cs:2958-2959`, `:2933-2935`), the steer half the flight chord sagitta `L²/(8R)` from `ComputeRouteRadius` / `Circumradius3D` (`:3255-3280`) against `TrackHalfWidth` less half the bounding box (`:519-520`); graded rather than switched; it gates only `SlowBefore`; and it never sums with the crest, it takes the smaller effect.

**Staging, so behaviour ships last.** Stage 0 is the detector plus an `Options.ShowBumps` HUD and no behaviour. Stage 1 runs the prediction and the window always-on with the per-car gravity and a full HUD - predicted against actual airtime, the gravity used, the lane error at touchdown, the severity - and that stage is what sets the metre baselines, the height floor and the hysteresis count, and what falsifies the estimate. Stage 2 wires `AirOnly`, stage 3 `SlowBefore`.

**The option is eleven sites** (the enum, the current-value field, the menu item, the `LoadSettings` read, the `SettingsRepair` key, the `Options` toggle, `DebugToggles`, the checkbox, the two migrations and the draw branch). The enum is positional and persistence is name-based, so a missed site is a silent default or an unreachable setting, never an error.

**The surface normal is free and currently thrown away.** `GetSurfaceHash` (`AutosportRacingSystem.cs:2291-2309`) already fetches the hit position and the **surface normal** from the shape test and returns only the material hash — so the normal costs no extra native, and its pitch is the local grade measured on the real collision surface rather than differenced from a route polyline that `SaveRoute` rounds to two decimals (`AutosportRacingSystem.TrackFile.cs:142`). That is a better prospect for locating where a ramp ends, and the same load-time sweep that serves the material map can carry it: one pass for material, grade and lip. Open for the design pass: whether the normal replaces the raw-Z differencing or only refines the lip, how a curved or ridged hit is guarded, how the sweep is cadenced in metres, and the fact that `didhit` is not checked today.
**The design pass verdict on the normal: refine, not replace.** A normal is a plane and carries no curvature while a lip is a curvature event, so the detector structure stands — two rungs, a *difference* across a baseline, and run persistence — and what changes is the **sample source**: the rounded polyline becomes the real collision surface for the slope, while position, distance and the run width stay on the polyline, so the crest path (which reads `TrackPoints[].Position`, `TrackLoader.cs:381`) and the bump path still agree about *where*. The departure angle gains little — the rounding noise at a 2 m rung is a fraction of a degree against a departure of several — so the whole claim lives in the **lip onset**, where the curvature noise is a fifth to a half of the 1 G launch threshold and the mesh normal may substitute a larger face-discontinuity noise of its own. Hence the rule: normal-first where it agrees with the polyline, raw-Z as fallback and tiebreaker, the more conservative hazard where they disagree, and never a silent override; plus `BumpMinRunNodes` mirroring the crest''s own `minRun`, so one node or one ray cannot manufacture a lip. The grade must be the **longitudinal** projection — the total tilt `acos(n·û)` folds a bank into pitch. One coarse sweep cannot serve both consumers (material wants zones, a 2-3 m lip wants per-node), so the apparatus is two-tiered: the free raw-Z pass nominates candidate lips, the sweep probes only those in a small ray cluster, and the material survey keeps its zone cadence — low thousands of natives at load, none per tick, with the collision request once per area rather than per ray. `GetSurfaceHash` becomes a `Try` that gates on `didhit` **and** the result status, takes the ignore entity as a parameter instead of hardcoding the player, and returns normal and hit position. This is a Stage 1 instrument question: the HUD shows both instruments side by side and the actual airtime decides, and if they land within noise raw-Z wins because it is free.
**They are different in kind, so they fail differently.** The height difference *infers* a slope by comparing two positions: its baseline decides what it can see — long enough to hide a short lip, short enough to drown in the two-decimal rounding — and it is what carries *shape* across a distance, which is what a lip is. The normal''s angle *is* the local slope with no baseline at all, which is what locates the lip, but it is one triangle''s plane, so it steps at mesh edges and cannot speak about curvature. Agreement between them is therefore real evidence rather than a formality, and disagreement is a data-quality flag. **Consequence:** this is the same measurement the crest onset asks for — where the track stops being flat and starts curving down — so if the second instrument earns its place on ramps it deserves the same question on the crest, as a separate change with its own drive, since the crest path is already built and still undriven. **Decision (driver's): the normal is the ramp instrument and altitude is the crest instrument** — for a ramp the detector differences the normal's own angle across the short rung, so the lip is where that angle stops climbing, and the crest keeps its altitude-difference detector exactly as built. That matches the two scales (a point slope with no baseline for a short lip, a wide-baseline shape for a long crest) and it leaves the crest path untouched, so nothing already built needs re-verifying when the ramp work lands.
**The driver''s caution: the normal is *too* precise.** A point measurement is fragile to exactly what sits on a road - a rock, a kerb lip, debris, a repaired patch, or the collision mesh''s own tessellation - so one reading must never be able to manufacture a ramp. The guards, in order: probe a small ray cluster per node and take the **median**, so one outlier cannot define a node; require the change to **persist over consecutive nodes** (`BumpMinRunNodes`, mirroring the crest''s own `minRun`); require the hit inside the drivable width and within a Z tolerance of the route; reject an implausible grade band; cross-check against the altitude difference and take the more conservative hazard; cap the implied severity. If the angle is still jumpy after the median, the honest knob is to **widen the rung** the angle is differenced over - a longer baseline averages a rock out at the cost of reading a short lip more weakly. Open for the design pass: the baseline itself, and the signature that actually separates a rock from a lip. **A hook already waits for this**: `CornerPoint.RequiresEarlyBrake` and `RampEndNode` (`DataStructures.cs:381-382`) are declared and *read* by the braking plan at `AutosportRacingSystem.cs:2951`, which prefers `RampEndNode` as the brake target, but nothing has ever written them - a half-built ramp feature, and the natural consumer for the slow-before behaviour. Stage 0 leaves them unset on purpose, so nothing moves yet.
**The parameters, and a correction to the rung logic (task-14).** The instrument stops being node-keyed: resample the route into **stations every metre** (`BumpProbeSpacingMeters`), which decouples the detector from a node spacing that runs 0.88-2.39 m (`AGENTS-STEERING.md:279`). The rungs in metres: departure tangent `RampAngleBaselineMeters` 2.0, persistence `BumpMinRunMeters` 2.0, lip onset `BumpLipBaselineMeters` 3.0, longest rise still counted a lip `BumpMaxRunMeters` 8.0, against the crest''s ~12 m window (`TrackLoader.cs:494`, `window = 6`) - so the ordering is 2 <= 2 <= 3 < 8 < 12 and the two instruments stay disjoint on the same stretch. **The correction**: the departure angle needs a *shorter* rung, not the car-length one first proposed, because a rung spanning past the lip returns a mean grade below the lip tangent and so *under-predicts* the launch, which is the dangerous direction; the onset read is a difference of two pitches and wants the longer rung to average triangle noise. Persistence equals the departure rung by design: the shortest feature worth measuring is the shortest run worth requiring.

**The rock case is already covered, with numbers - no new machinery.** The division of labour is explicit: the cluster spread rejects a ray that hit something off-surface, persistence rejects a one-station event, altitude and width reject an off-route hit, the grade band rejects a face that is not road. A 2-3 m lip spans 2-3 stations and passes; a rock spike lasts one station and dies; a tessellation step has no rise-then-fall reversal; a repaired patch runs past 8 m. Three rays at ±0.5 m cannot all be fooled by a footprint under about a metre, and a larger rock all three do hit sits above the route Z and cannot hold a two-station run at road level. A rock of 2 m or more, planar, at road level with a plausible grade **should** be accepted: the car genuinely launches off it, and the gates exist to kill false ramps, not real geometry. The cluster is 3 rays at -0.5 / 0 / +0.5 m, median of the **projected angle scalar** rather than the raw normal, widening to 5 rays at ±1.0 m; six natives per station and none per tick. Fallback order if the angle stays jumpy: widen the cluster, then raise persistence to 3 m (a real cost to 2-2.5 m lips), then defer to the altitude instrument, then fail closed - an unstable read never becomes a value.
**The lane-aware pass supersedes two things above, and it is the answer to alternating bumps.** The driver has seen tracks where the trouble returns off the tarmac: one zone rough on the left while the right is clean, the rough side alternating, and the cars unaware. That invalidates the centre-line footprint (three rays within a metre of the centre cannot see a one-lane bump when the drivable width is up to five metres a side) and the scalar bump geometry (one lip angle per station is not enough when the same station launches a car on one side and not the other). It does **not** invalidate the ramp physics, the per-car gravity, the ballistic flight, the prediction-arms/truth-decides split, the three behaviours, the severity formula or the crest path, which is longitudinal and stays out of it. The replacement is a **lateral profile per station**: bands of 1.3-1.5 m across the full drivable width, computed per station from that station''s own `TrackHalfWidth` because it varies per node, keyed by arc distance in metres with a lateral offset in metres, published as a `BumpStation` list beside `ARS.Bumps`. Two tiers are required laterally or the ray count is unaffordable: a coarse sweep, one ray per band at the material survey''s cadence, nominates a band whose residual from the **cross-band median** exceeds the cluster spread and persists two metres, and only those candidates are then densified. Low tens of thousands of natives at load, none per tick.
**What the scan actually nominates (a review pass measured it over the sixteen tracks in `Dist`).** Not every accepted feature is a lip that drops away: on El Burro Rally and Oct1 **none** drops - the road keeps climbing and only the *grade* eases - so the scan nominates **short convex bends**, of which a dropping lip is one kind. That is the right thing for a launch (the criterion is curvature, not a cliff), and it has three consequences worth keeping: the disjointness with the crest instrument holds by **scale** rather than by shape, since these run 2-8 m against the crest window of about 12 m, so the min-not-sum rule now has a real instance instead of a hypothetical one; the read-time flight must be **walked against the road**, because a car can leave a continuing climb and land on one; and the run lengths are **node-quantised** (about half a node, so roughly half a metre on these tracks and over a metre on the coarsest), until Stage 1 resamples to metre stations as the design already asks. The detector's own drop floor **caps** the speed a feature demands, which is what makes the accepted set launchable at racing speed - it is not a minimum. The floor, not the geometry, is what tunes the scanner's precision, and the rise walk reads its grade on the longer rung and passes over one failing sample: measured over the sixteen tracks, that walk nominates 89 features against 77 for the single-sample one, with about 40 new and 28 gone, so the walk's own parameters decide *which* features reach the drive rather than how many. Every new feature has a positive rise height and passes the gates. The marginal ones worth eyeballing on the drive are those sitting at a gate: El Burro Rally 1237 (departure exactly at the floor), Around Grapeseed 651 and 1646, and Offroad Hardcore 791, whose run spans a local dip while still netting a rise. The **launch resolves from the band the car will be in at the lip** - its planned lateral offset, not its current one - so the detector stays car-independent and identical across the three modes while the consumer carries the car state, exactly as gravity does. Preferring the clean side is a *plan* change: its own setting, its own late stage after the three behaviours, and it is inserted **on the high-speed lane before** the corner and avoidance overrides (`Racer.cs:521`), so the apex line, the avoidance pick, the rival walls and the debug lock all overwrite it in their existing order - subordination is structural rather than a set of rules to keep in sync. When a car must take the rough side to pass, the rival walls win by construction and the car still arms and slows, because arming follows the band it is truly in.
**Only a drive settles** these: that the short lip really is averaged out of the crest window, the metre-baseline noise floor, the point-mass flight and its error envelope, that a flagged car's real airtime scales as the ARS model expects, that the ride-height margin fires on a real launch, and that the sagitta tracks the line loss the game actually produces.
